# Vantage guide

## Start here

Vantage is an Ashita v4 addon. Load it with `/addon load vantage`. The [install notes](INSTALL.md) show how to copy it into your game folder. Vantage draws its own displays and leaves the game's own windows as they are.

Type `/vantage` or `/vg` to open the main page. The self-buff, self-debuff, and target timer panels start enabled. Party members' timers, Watched enemy timers, the recast panels and the buffs beside the game's party list are optional. Party members' timers and Watched enemy timers start as bars, which name whose effect each row is. Timer panels normally hide when they're empty. Show samples fills every panel with effects that are clearly made up, so you can arrange them. Samples never count as something Vantage saw in the game, and they never set off alerts. A sample icon panel wears a small SAMPLE tag. While the settings window is open, samples draw over it, so it never hides them. Clicks still go to the settings. The sample under the mouse, its tag included, steps back behind the window for as long as the mouse is on it. That way you can see the settings under it and its tooltip. A sample under one of the window's tooltips or open lists steps back too. In Arrange on screen every display draws as usual, under its outline.

The window always opens on the main page, and you set most things there. The main page goes in the order you set things up: **What to show**, **How it looks** and **Moving**. Every change on it is one Undo step, except **Hold Shift to see through this window**. That tick only changes how this window works. **Edit** beside a display opens that display's own page. The bar at the top of every page has **Undo**, **Redo**, the search box, **Hide list**, **Timer durations**, **Profiles** and **Help**, and **Mute alerts** at its end. While alerts are muted, it reads **Unmute alerts**.

A line shows at the top only while something needs a hand. It shows when timer panels are paused (**Resume**), when the Hide list is paused (**Use it again**), when Party cooldowns has nowhere to show (**Show them in a list**), when the ready pulse has no recast panel to play on (**Use a spot of its own**), while samples show (**End samples**), while Arrange on screen is on (**Done arranging**), or when `/vg hide` has hidden the displays (**Show**).

1. **What to show.** **Quick setup for a role** at the top sets all of this for a role at once (see [Quick setup for a role](#quick-setup-for-a-role)). Or tick what you want. The displays come in groups. **On you** has Self buffs, Self debuffs, **Recasts** (**Spells**, **Abilities** and **Items**), **Ready pulse** and **Pet**. **Target and enemies** has Target timers, Watched enemy timers and **Overhead icons**. **Party** has **Party buffs**, **Party cooldowns**, **Show the distance to each member** and **Phantom Roll**. Then comes **Alerts**. Each has its tick and **Edit** on one row. When the window is wide enough, **On you** sits beside **Target and enemies**, with **Alerts** under that, and **Party** takes the whole width below them. **Party buffs** and **Party cooldowns** each have a tick for every place they show: **In a list**, **Beside the party list** and **Over heads**. For Party buffs those are Party members' timers, Party list and Overhead icons, and its **Edit** opens Party list. **Show the distance to each member** turns Party list on too, alone there if Party list was off. **Overhead icons** draws buffs and debuffs above heads in the game world. **Party cooldowns** counts the two-hours, key abilities and Utsusemi Vantage saw your party use. **Phantom Roll** shows a roll while you or a party member rolls. **Pet** shows your pet while you have one. A display stays empty when there is no matching information to show. **Alerts** says **Muted** and has **Unmute** while alerts are muted.
2. **While playing**, at the end of What to show, holds what every display does as you play. That's **Hide Vantage in cutscenes, on the map and when the game hides its menus**, **Hide timer panels while I type**, **Show details when I point at a timer**, **Follow the cursor while I am choosing a target**, **Blink before an effect ends, starting at** (see [Expiring effect icons](#expiring-effect-icons)), **Color each countdown by how sure Vantage is of its time**, **Replace the game's buff icons** (see [Replace the game's buff icons](#replace-the-games-buff-icons)) and **Replace the game's pet bar** (see [Pet](#pet)). Party list and Overhead icons hide in cutscenes, on the map and with the game's menus either way, because they sit on what the game hides. **Chat line colors**, a fold at its end, sets the colors of Vantage's own lines in the chat log.
3. **How it looks.** Pick a **Skin**. It applies at once to every display and this window, with its fonts and artwork. It keeps your sizes, positions, filters, locks, the colors you set for one effect and row text colors. When a pick would replace a display's own look, the page names those displays first and waits for **Apply** or **Cancel**. **Font** sets the font for every display and this window. If this window can't use that font, it keeps its plain one, and a line under **Font** says so. **Text size** is the size of effect names and countdowns. **Icons** panels go by **Icon size**, which sits right under **Text size** while your timer panels are all on Icons. **Size** has **1x**, **1.5x** and **2x** buttons and a slider. A button sets the size. In the same Undo step, it also re-spaces the timer panels, alert notices and the Pet and Phantom Roll displays that are still where they started, so they fit the new size clear of the game's windows and chat log. Every display you moved keeps its position. After you turn a timer panel on or move one, the alert notices, other timer panels and the Pet and Phantom Roll displays can keep their positions too, as Vantage can't always tell them from ones you moved. Layouts, fields and colors never reset. The slider only sets the size.
4. Under How it looks, five folds start closed. **Artwork** holds **Icons**, with **Reload icons** and **Open icons folder** under it, and **Recast icons**, and on Original FFXI and XI View also **Gauges** and all eight client window types under **Window frame**. **Sizes** holds **Icon size**, **Row height**, **Space between rows** and **Heading size**. While your timer panels are all on Icons, **Icon size** isn't in it, and a line says which of these reach them. **Boxes** holds **Opacity**, **Corner rounding**, **Background**, **Border** and **No boxes or headings**, which takes **Background** and **Border** away. **Text effects** holds the countdown and other text effects for every display (see [Countdown text](#countdown-text)). **Colors** has the shared colors, **Lit icons** with **Glow around them**, and **Back to the skin's colors**.
5. **Moving.** A line says which displays are unlocked. One button sets every display's lock. It reads **Unlock all**. Once a display that is on is unlocked, it reads **Lock all**. The button covers the timer and recast panels, Party list, alert notices, the Party cooldowns list, the ready pulse spot and the Phantom Roll and Pet displays. Each action supports Undo. Hold Shift until handles appear. Then drag the center to move a display, or drag an edge or corner to resize and wrap it. Individual locks stay above the scrolling settings when there is room. In short menus, scroll the page to reach these controls. Locked displays stay fixed even while you hold Shift. With the pointer on one, a note says where to unlock it. Every display starts locked. **Arrange on screen** outlines every display so you can pick and move it, locked or not (see [Arrange on screen](#arrange-on-screen)). **Show samples** puts samples on every display that is on. It doesn't turn anything on. While samples show, **End samples** at the top of the page ends them all. **Snap while moving** starts off. It makes displays snap as you drag them, and the bar of Arrange on screen picks which ones snap and to what. Under **This window**, **Hold Shift to see through this window** hides this window while you hold Shift, so you can place displays under it, and **Settings text size** sets the size of its text.

A look you set for one display stays until you change it. Vantage doesn't reapply anything on its own.

The timer and recast panels, the ready pulse, Party list, Overhead icons, Party cooldowns, Phantom Roll, Pet and Alerts open from **Edit** on their main page rows. **Hide list**, **Timer durations**, **Profiles** and **Help** at the top open **Hide list**, **Timer durations**, **Profiles and sharing** and **Help**. Each page but the main page has a back button and its name at the top. The back button goes back the way you came, one place at a time, and names where it goes, such as **< Main page**. The bar at the top stays the same on every page. The X on the title bar or Esc closes the window. When you change pages, the new page starts at the top. Search jumps to its match. The window always opens on the main page. The **Hide list** hides an effect on the timer panels, Party list, Overhead icons and Pet. Each panel has a **Filters** tab for its own rules and ordering. Search also finds controls on editor tabs that aren't open. If a control shows only while something else is on, search finds that switch instead. A word ending in s finds the word without it too, so `targets` finds **Target timers**. A result with nothing to light up on its page opens the page and clears the search. **Previous match**, **Next match** and the count of matches show only while the page has a match.

**Edit** beside a timer or recast panel, the ready pulse, Party list, Overhead icons, Party cooldowns, Phantom Roll, Pet or Alerts on the main page opens its editor. Its top line has the back button and the display's name, which is a list of every display. Pick another there to open it, on the same tab when it has one. After the name come **On**, **Show samples** and **Locked**, on the next line when the window is narrow. Overhead icons have no **Locked**, because you don't drag anything over a head. Alerts and the ready pulse have no **Show samples**, because they have no sample to show. **On** changes only that panel. Hiding Target keeps Self buffs and Self debuffs visible, even when you target yourself. While every status and recast panel is paused, a line at the top of the main page says so. Each panel keeps its visibility choice. Turning on an individual panel, or clicking **Resume** on the main page, resumes the shared display switch. **Show samples** shows samples on that display only. It doesn't turn anything on. While the display is off, it says to turn the display on first. Unticking **On** ends them. The main page's **Show samples**, or `/vg preview on`, shows them on every display that is on. While any display shows them, that button is **End samples**, and it and `/vg preview` end them everywhere. Unticking one panel's **Show samples** ends them there and leaves the others.

When the display isn't on the screen now, a line under the top line says why. That's when the timer panels are paused (**Resume**), when `/vg hide` has hidden the displays (**Show**), when its When to show rules hide it, or when it has nothing to show. While it's unlocked, a line says how to move it.

Then come the tabs. **Show** is for what it lists and what each row shows. **Size and place** is for how big it is, where it sits and what it moves with. **Look** is for its heading, box, bars, colors and row text. **Filters** is for what it hides, pins, orders and renames. There's also **When to show**. Vantage draws only the open tab, and search looks through them all. A display has no tab it has nothing for. Each panel remembers the tab you left it on. At the foot of every tab are **Reset this tab** and **Reset all of this display**. **Reset this tab** puts back what that tab sets, in one Undo step. On a recast panel's **Show**, **Show as**, **Icons show** and the recast tints are one for every recast panel, so neither reset changes them. **Reset all of this display** puts the whole display back as it started, and it asks first. On a timer or recast panel's **Look**, **Filters** and **When to show**, **Copy this tab to** gives another panel of that kind the same settings, or **Every other panel**. From a recast panel's **Filters**, the other kinds take **Show only names containing** and **Only recasts with a known time**, and keep their own lists. A **Look** copy between Self buffs or Self debuffs and another kind of panel leaves **Who it is on** as each has it. A lock only stops Shift and drag. **Arrange on screen** and the numbers on **Size and place** still move a locked display. Browsing these tabs doesn't write settings or use Undo.

If a settings page runs into an error, the window closes until you open it again. Vantage puts back the change the page was making and reports the error in chat. `/vg` then says the window closed after an error and that `/vg` opens it again. While your visibility options hide the panels (an event, the map or a hidden interface), `/vg` says the window shows when they end. The window follows those options together with the panels.

### Quick setup for a role

**Quick setup for a role** is at the top of **What to show** on the main page. It turns on the displays a role wants and turns the rest off. The list opens on the one that suits your main job. It reads `(in use)` beside the one your settings match. Picking one changes nothing until you press **Apply**.

- **Apply** does it in one Undo step. It also puts samples on every display that's on, so you can see what you got. **End samples**, at the top of the main page, takes them off.
- To keep one as a profile, **Apply** it, then click **Save as new** on **Profiles and sharing**. To load it on WHM, pick it there, pick WHM under **Load this profile when I change to** and click **Link**. A saved profile holds its whole look, so a look you change later doesn't reach it until you overwrite it.
- `/vg starter healer` applies one from the chat line, with no samples.

All six turn on Self buffs, Self debuffs, Spell recasts, Ability recasts, the Pet display and overhead icons, in the recast layout you chose. The recast panels and the Pet display stay hidden while they have nothing to show.

A starter keeps your skin, fonts, colors, sizes, positions, locks, hide rules, your own alert rules, the party list **Countdowns** and distance, job links, saved profiles, item recasts, and the overhead **Other players**, **Other players' pets** and **You** ticks. The parts of the Pet display and the lights on overhead icons stay as they are too, once Pet or Healer has turned them on. Each starter turns **Only effects from job abilities** off on Party members' timers and Party list, and **Include me** and **One row for a cast on several members** off on Party members' timers, so they show what the table says.

Starters add alert rules and never take one away. They skip a rule you already have for that effect and event, whether it's on or off, and whatever it's called or covers. A starter with rules of its own turns **Alerts** on. **Silenced** tells you when you're silenced. **Sleep ending** warns 10 seconds before Sleep, Sleep II or Lullaby runs out on anything.

### Hide selected buffs and debuffs

Click **Hide list** at the top of the window, or type `/vg filters`. **Edit the list** on a display's **Filters** tab opens it too. Vantage hides what's on this list across the timer panels and Party list. Overhead icons and Pet use it too, and each can stop using it with **Use the Hide list** on its own **Filters** tab. The list doesn't touch recasts. It starts empty. While the list is paused, each page that uses it says so, with **Use it again**.

Pick an effect under **Effect**, which reads **Choose an effect** until you do. Type part of its name in the box at the top of the list to find it. Then click **Hide it everywhere**. Each effect on the list shows by name under **Hide list**. **Remove** takes one off. Hiding Level Sync removes its buff icon. Hiding Copy Image hides it no matter how many shadows are left.

**Hide only for some players or jobs** starts closed. It narrows a rule. **Add a rule** adds one that's turned off. It goes on the effect chosen above, or on **Any effect** when none is chosen. Set it up, then tick **Use this rule**. **Rule** picks which you're changing. A rule that's off, or on **Any effect**, shows only here. Choose an effect or **Any effect**, **Buffs or debuffs**, and any mix of these:

- **Player name:** Type an exact name. Case and spaces around it don't matter. Leave it blank for anyone. This is the player with the effect, not its caster.
- **Main job:** Pick a main job or **Any main job**. If the client doesn't give a member's job, such as for a distant or out-of-zone member, that member doesn't match a job-specific rule.
- **Who:** Pick everyone, only you, your party including you, both other alliance parties, or Alliance 1 / Alliance 2 individually. You can also pick Enemies, Other players (outside your party and alliance) or Pets. Those match wherever they show, on overhead icons too. Enemies and Pets have no player's name or job. Picking one clears those, and their two rows go away.

Every condition in one rule must match. Any enabled rule that matches hides the effect. That includes effects that local display filters pin or include. For example, Signet + WAR + My party hides Signet only on WARs in your party. Any effect + Debuffs + a player name hides that player's debuff icons and timers. Tracking and alerts continue normally. Effects with no known duration still appear unless a rule hides them.

**Pause this list** shows everything on it again without deleting anything. The main page then says it's paused. **Use it again**, there or here, puts it back. **Clear the list** takes every effect and rule off it. Undo restores changes. Full profiles and shared presentation include these rules. Placement-only sharing and skins keep them unchanged.

Vantage keeps its settings in `config/addons/vantage`. This guide calls that location the **settings folder**. Each character has a separate settings file. Vantage stores profiles and optional shared placement beside those character folders. Custom fonts, sounds, icon sets, caches, and reports use this same location. Replacing the addon folder leaves these files alone.

## Read a timer

- **Server:** The game sent a current timer for a status on you. The number is the time left. The original total and the elapsed time appear only when Vantage saw the effect being applied. So if you load Vantage midway through a buff, it doesn't make up the buff's original duration.
- **Estimated:** Vantage saw the effect being applied and has a supported duration model for it. These rows use estimate styling, and their details point out the uncertainty. Caster equipment, random durations, resistance, or later server adjustments may change the actual duration. Some estimates are a range, shown as two times such as **2m - 8m**: the time left if the effect lasts its shortest, and if it lasts its longest. A monster's move whose time goes by its TP or a roll shows one, since the game sends neither, and so does a charmed monster's or a jug pet's move. So does another player's bar-element spell, whose time goes by their Enhancing Magic skill, from 2m 30s up to 3m 31s, a level 75 RDM's time at their skill cap. Another player's buff song runs from what it lasts with no instrument up to what the best level 75 instrument gives, doubled while their party listing shows Troubadour. Hymnus and Mazurka double again under Soul Voice. An alliance member's songs count as with neither. Their Requiem, Elegy, Threnody and Lullaby on a mob count the same way from each song's base, up to what the best level 75 instrument gives, doubled under Troubadour. A monster's song is its base. Another player's Flee shows 30 to 45 seconds, since Rogue's Poulaines add 15. Feral Howl's Terror shows 2 to 10 seconds. The bar and the row go by the longest. Party list's icons, the Pet display's icons and the overhead icons show only the longest. Party list's tooltip shows the longest too, and the Pet display's tooltip shows both.
- **No countdown:** The effect is present or Vantage saw it, but Vantage has no usable current duration for it. It shows its icon, or a bar with a plain track, in its normal look. That doesn't mean the effect is permanent. An estimate that ran out with no wear-off message stays like that until it leaves.

Hover a row for its evidence, caster when known, effect ID, and available timing details. Duplicate icons stay separate. When a timer reaches zero, Vantage doesn't remove the row or fire a removal alert. That needs evidence from a current status snapshot or a removal Vantage sees. Old observations that were never confirmed go away quietly.

Rows follow Phoenix's own rules for repeated effects. Recasting a buff you already have refreshes its row. When the server lists the old and the new copy together for a moment, the old copy leaves quietly and nothing is removed or gained. A song of the same kind and tier refreshes its copy in place rather than adding one. For example, an Etude refreshes an Etude of the same tier, and a Minuet refreshes the same Minuet. When a bard sings past their song limit, the song of theirs that ends first is the one that drops. When you die, your rows stay until the server clears them. Vantage credits Weakness to whoever raised you.

Self snapshots cover the status slots the client exposes. Party snapshots cover members of your own party in the same zone, when their icon update is available. They don't give exact timers. Other players, alliance members, pets, and enemies can appear through what Vantage sees, but Vantage has no complete, guaranteed list of their effects. Changing targets doesn't find effects that were applied out of sight.

### Hits on a mob

Some debuffs on a mob come with no word from the game at all. A weapon skill is the main case: when Shield Break lands Evasion Down, the game sends only the damage. The same goes for a jug pet's, an automaton's or a charmed monster's move, Weapon Bash, Shield Bash, Chi Blast with Penance, Jump under Spirit Surge, Tomahawk, Feral Howl's Terror, a blue magic hit, a damaging blood pact, an item used on a mob, Ice Spikes' Paralysis, Shock Spikes' 3-second Stun and the Evasion Down from Feint. Vantage takes each of those as put on when the action did what it needs to: a hit for damage, or the action's own message. A resist looks exactly the same, so now and then one shows that never landed. It shows on every display like any other debuff, with the action's usual time as an estimate, or no countdown where that isn't known. A Break lasts three to five minutes by TP, which the game doesn't send, so it counts down three. Your automaton's Shield Bash gives Slow only with a Hammermill on it and an Earth Maneuver up, and Vantage checks both. It lasts 30, 50 or 70 seconds by how many Earth Maneuvers you have, or 70 under Overdrive. Another player's automaton isn't checked, so its Slow shows 30 to 70 seconds. Tomahawk's effect and the one a Bottle of Hysteroanima gives have no name or icon in the game, so they show as **Tomahawk** and **Hysteria**, each with its item's picture. When that time is up it goes, since the game never said it was on, and one with no countdown goes after three minutes. It never renews an effect the mob already shows, or goes on over one that keeps it off, as Haste does Slow. When it shows, an effect it takes off goes, as a Break's Defense Down takes off Defense Boost. Your own wear-off message ends it early when it comes.

A mob's Sleep, Sleep II and Lullaby leave at the first hit Vantage sees on it for more than 0 damage, the same way Phoenix takes them off. The hits that count are melee, ranged and weapon skill hits, Barrage and Sange shots, damaging spells and abilities, drains, blood pacts, added effects and skillchains. Phoenix lands every player sleep and lullaby as Sleep, so they all behave alike. A miss, a hit for 0 and a resist change nothing. The removal counts for **Effect removed / wore off** alerts.

Bind stays after a hit but loses its countdown. Phoenix breaks it at 95 hits in 100, so there's no way to know its time. If it breaks, its caster gets the wear-off. A counter on a bound mob's attack is a hit on it too. Spikes don't count. Phoenix deals their damage without waking a mob or breaking Bind.

Damage ticks from Poison, Dia, Bio and the like wake a sleeper too. But the game sends no message for a tick, so only the sleep's caster sees it leave, through its wear-off. Sleepga from Shiva and Nightmare from Diabolos send no wear-off to anyone, so a hit is the only sign. Nightmare also takes Dia and Bio off the mob, with no word of it, and puts on a Bio of its own for the sleep's time. That Bio's ticks don't wake the mob, a hit leaves it on, and no Dia or Bio goes on over it while it lasts. Vantage shows it that way, whoever's Diabolos it was. Effects on you and your party follow their own listings. Vantage counts these hits only on mobs and your pet.

Nothing can act while it's asleep, petrified, stunned or in terror. So once Vantage sees a mob or your pet finish a swing, a spell, an ability or a move, any Sleep, Petrification, Stun and Terror on it leave. That's often the only sign: when a monster's move puts your pet to sleep, the game tells nobody but that monster when it wears off.

A mob's Stoneskin leaves at the first hit Vantage sees on it for more than 0 damage. Phoenix takes Stoneskin's share out of a hit before it shows the damage, so a hit for more than 0 means none was left. Metallic Body soaks only 25 damage on most mobs. The game sends no one the wear-off for a mob's own buff, so a hit is the only sign. The same hits count as for a sleep, but not Weapon Bash, which Phoenix deals straight to HP, or a skillchain the mob absorbs. Spikes that hurt it for more than 0 count too, as Phoenix shows their damage after Stoneskin's share. Your spikes and a party member's count while Blaze, Ice, Shock or Dread Spikes or Gallant's Roll shows on you or them. Spikes from gear show their damage from before Stoneskin, so they don't count. Damage ticks use Stoneskin up without a message, and a hit that uses up exactly what was left shows 0. Then the icon stays until the next hit for damage. Your pet's Stoneskin goes the same way, and at a mob's spikes too.

### Shadows and Third Eye

The game lists buffs for your own party only. So on a mob or an alliance member, Vantage follows Copy Image, Blink and Third Eye itself, from what it sees.

Copy Image is one effect, whatever its icon shows. So a filter or an alert on it holds no matter how many shadows are left. On a mob or an alliance member, Vantage counts the shadows, starting from the spell. Ichi gives 3, Ni 4 and San 5. But Ni gives one less to Ark Angel HM and Shikaree X, and to an alliance member whose main job isn't Ninja. When the game hasn't sent their job, Vantage counts them as a Ninja. If that leaves a shadow too many, it goes at the next hit that gets through. Vantage takes one off for each shadow it sees soak up a hit, a spell or a counter. The icon goes with the last one. A hit for damage that gets through means none were left, so the icon goes then too. A weapon skill with Sneak Attack or Trick Attack can use up shadows without the game saying how many. Some attacks take every shadow at once without a word. So the count can read high until the next hit. A Seether's Occultation shows as Copy Image and counts down 5 minutes, though the game calls it Blink. The game doesn't say how many shadows it gave, so it has no count and goes at a hit that gets through.

Blink is counted like Copy Image, under its own icon, 2 from the spell and 4 from a blue mage's Zephyr Mantle. A hit for damage can get past Blink, so one that gets through doesn't end it. Its last shadow or its time does. The monster move called Zephyr Mantle gives a random count, so Vantage doesn't count that one.

Third Eye counts down its 30 seconds, and Seigan its 5 minutes. On a mob or an alliance member, Third Eye goes when Vantage sees it anticipate an attack, sees a swing land on them, or sees them cast Utsusemi. With Seigan up, it can stay after an attack it anticipates, and the game doesn't say when it does. So its icon stays until its time is up or a swing lands. Some area attacks from mobs take Third Eye off without a word. When that happens, its icon stays until its time is up.

## Panels

Open a panel with **Edit** on the main page. Each panel has its own position, width, maximum height, visible row limit, layout, order, bar direction and filters. Self buffs, Self debuffs and Target timers start as horizontal icon rows. Party members' timers, Watched enemy timers and the recast panels start as bars.

An effect goes by the game's own name for it. The game spells many debuffs in lowercase, such as paralysis, slow and silence, and Vantage shows those with capitals.

**Bars** keep rows short. **Expanded bars** make room for more on each row. **Text** shows each name and its countdown, with no icon or bar. **Icons** shows just the icon and its known countdown, with no background, heading, name or gauge. Effects with no countdown and Level Sync show only their icon. Countdown bold, shadow, outline, fonts and client artwork still apply. Bar field choices stay saved when you switch back. Countdowns start as centered text directly below the icon, at half the icon's rendered size. Their own size, position and gap are under **Countdowns on icons** on the **Size and place** tab.

**Fills**, on the same tab, picks which way a panel fills in every layout. The choices are **To the right, then down**, **To the left, then down**, **Down, then to the right** and **Down, then to the left**. The panel's width and height decide when its contents wrap. Names, timers and bar fill stay readable in their normal direction. **Column width** shows on **Size and place** while the rows sit side by side, on a panel other than **Icons** that fills to the right or left, or whose rows go into columns. Each column is at least that wide, so a wider one makes fewer columns. In a panel that fills down, rows that don't fit in its height go on in another column. ### Mob buffs you can remove

Target timers and Watched enemy timers can light a mob's buff when your job and level can take it off now. They use the same checks as the overhead icons, and you need one of these. Dispel needs RDM 32, or /RDM with a main job of 64 and up. Magic Finale needs BRD 33, or /BRD at 66 and up. Both have to be learned. Blank Gaze needs BLU 38, learned and set. The game sends your blue magic set when you zone in and when you change it, so after `/addon reload vantage` Blank Gaze counts once you zone or change your set. Dark Shot needs COR 40, with a Dark Card or Trump Card in your inventory, a Marksmanship weapon (gun or crossbow) and its ammo. Level Sync counts. A lit icon gets a bright border in **Light color**, with a glow behind it while **Glow around lit icons** is on. A bar row with no icon gets the border around its bar. The switch, **Light up buffs you can remove**, starts off. You'll find it on each panel's **Show** tab. Once it's on, **Glow around lit icons** and **Light color** show on its **Look** tab. The glow starts on. A mob's debuffs and players' buffs never light here. Nor does a mob's Arrow Shield, which no removal takes off on Phoenix. While a mob has a Magic Shield, Phoenix turns every removal away, so none of its buffs light until the shield's time is up. A Soulflayer's Immortal Shield is the one that doesn't, and it lights itself. Profiles and shared presentation carry each panel's choices.

### Debuffs you can remove

Self debuffs and Party members' timers can light a debuff the same way. On Phoenix that takes White Mage as your main or support job, at the spell's level, with the spell learned. The spells and their levels are Poisona (6), Paralyna (9), Blindna (14), Silena (19), Cursna (29), Erase (32), Viruna (34) and Stona (39). Stona's level is past what a /WHM reaches. Level Sync counts. **Light up debuffs you can remove** starts off on both panels. It's on the same **Show** tab, with its own **Glow around lit icons** and **Light color** on **Look**.

A lit icon means you can try. Cursna only has a chance on Doom, and Erase takes off one debuff at random. So a lit debuff can stay on after the cast. Only your job, level and learned spells count. Items and abilities that remove debuffs light nothing, and a Silenced White Mage still sees the light.

### Replace the game's buff icons

**Replace the game's buff icons**, on the main page, starts off. With it on, the game's own buff and debuff icons are hidden, so Self buffs and Self debuffs are the only ones on screen. It needs both of those panels on. Whenever Vantage can't show your effects there, the game draws its own icons again. That happens while either panel is off, while the timer panels are paused, while samples show, after `/vg hide`, while Ashita's `/hideui` is on and while **When to show** hides both panels. It also happens if Vantage's panels stop drawing because of an error, until they draw again. While either panel is off, the timer panels are paused or samples show, a line under the tick says why. After `/vg hide`, a line at the top of the main page says the displays are hidden. An error that stops the panels is reported in chat. When your own rules hide the panels, the game's icons stay hidden with them, so no buff icons show at all. That covers the map and cutscenes, and **Hide timer panels while I type** while you type. While **When to show** hides both Self buffs and Self debuffs, the game draws its own icons again, except in **Arrange on screen**, which shows both panels. With only one of them hidden, the other still stands in, so the game's stay hidden.

You still cancel a buff with the game's own + menu. Open it and move through it with the same keys or controller buttons as always. Its cursor shows on Vantage's icon for the buff it's on, as a box in the accent color. With the same effect on you twice, like two Marches, the box is on the one the menu is on. Confirm cancels that buff. The game does the cancel, and Vantage sends nothing. Clicking an icon does nothing. When the + menu is on an effect no self panel shows, because a filter hides it or the panel has no room for it, a small line by Self buffs shows its name, like `+ menu: Protect`. Apart from that line, an effect your filters hide shows nowhere while this is on.

To do this, Vantage changes how the game draws its buff icons, in the game's memory, and keeps the game's + menu off screen. It puts both back when you turn this off or unload Vantage. Before it changes anything, it checks that this part of the game is exactly the one it knows. The first time after the game starts, it looks for that part over a few frames, and the game's icons stay until it has. If a game update changes that part, the game draws its own icons, and a line under the tick says so. The same goes when something else in the game has changed the game's menu mover: Vantage leaves it alone, and the line says that's why. The setting is each character's own. Profiles, exports and shared layouts don't carry it.

## Party list

Vantage can show buffs and debuffs beside the game's own party list. It shows a row of icons for each member of your party and of the other two alliance parties. The game's party and alliance windows stay as they are. Vantage reads where their member rows are and draws beside them. This display is **Party list**. **Beside the party list**, on the main page's **Party buffs** row, turns it on, and **Edit** on that row opens it. So does `/vg party`. It has the same top line and tabs as a timer panel.

**On** turns the buffs on or off together, and so do `/vg party show` and `/vg party hide`. If you turn them on with every group off, Your party turns on. The **Show** tab starts with **Groups**, a tick for each of **Your party**, **Alliance 1** and **Alliance 2**. Under them, each group that shows has a tick to put it in a panel of its own (see Size and place below). After those, you pick **Buffs and debuffs**, **Buffs only**, **Debuffs only** or **None**. An effect that is neither a buff nor a debuff, such as Level Sync, shows only with **Buffs and debuffs**. **None** shows no effects. It's for when you only want Party cooldowns or the distance beside the party list. The settings that only shape effects leave the page until you pick another one. Each change is one Undo step.

**Show samples** or `/vg party preview` adds sample effects beside the members the game shows now. It doesn't add sample members. `/vg party show` ends the samples, and so does unticking **On**. `/vg party show` also turns the buffs on, and Your party too if every group is off. It brings back displays hidden by `/vg hide` as well. While the buffs are off, **Show samples** says to turn them on first and changes nothing. Samples create no tracking or alert events.

Alliance rows show the effects Vantage sees land on alliance members. The game sends buff icons for your own party only. So an alliance member's row holds what Vantage saw cast on them, with estimated countdowns or none. It can miss what was cast out of sight. Copy Image there shows how many shadows are left (see **Shadows and Third Eye**).

**Light up debuffs you can remove**, on the **Show** tab, lights a debuff the way the panels do (see **Debuffs you can remove**). It starts off. While it's on, **Glow around lit icons** and **Light color** show on the **Look** tab. **Lit icons**, under **Colors** on the main page, sets them on every display at once. Erase doesn't reach the alliance rows, so a debuff only it removes stays unlit there.

Party changes support profiles, Undo, Redo, **Reset all of this display** and `/vg reset party_ui`.

### Show

**Countdowns**, on the **Show** tab, shows the time left on each party buff icon. It starts off, so at first the buffs are icons only.

**Show the distance to each member**, right after **Countdowns**, shows how far each member is from you in yalms, like 20.4. It starts off. It counts height, the way Phoenix checks range, so someone right above you can still be far. Beside the game's list the number gets a column of its own nearest the list, and each group's icons move out by its width, about 30 px at 1920x1080 on **Small**. The numbers are right-aligned, so the points line up. They use the countdowns' look, sized to the game's rows, and the countdowns' color at the buffs' **Opacity**. In a panel of its own, the number sits at the right end of the line with the member's name, in the text color. Your own row shows none. Nor does a member in another zone or their Mog House, or one the game isn't showing you, about 50 yalms off or 20 above or below you. Their buff icons keep coming either way. With **Show samples** on, each row gets a sample by its slot, 4.2, 9.6, 14.3, 20.4, 21.9 and 30.6, your own row too.

**Distance size** shows right under it while the distance is on and a group is beside the game's list, and sets how big the numbers are beside the game's list. **Small**, the default, is the size above. **Medium**, **Large** and **Largest** make the numbers as big as they can be up to 70, 80 and 90% of each row's height, so there's a little space between one member's number and the next. None of them is ever smaller than **Small**. With the Original FFXI and XI View fonts, that's the height of the digits. Any other font is measured by its whole line, which is taller than its digits, so its numbers come out smaller, and **Medium** can be the same as **Small**. The column widens with the size, up to about 40 to 50 px on **Largest** at 1920x1080, and the icons move out to make room. In a panel of its own the number keeps the name's text size.

**Warning color past** shows while the distance is on. Past it, the number turns your **Ending soon and alerts** color. **Spell range**, the default, is how far Cure, Raise, Haste, Protect, Shell, Regen, Refresh and the -na spells reach. On Phoenix that's 20 yalms plus 0.3 for a Tarutaru, 0.8 for a Galka or 0.4 for anyone else, once for each of you. So it's 20.8 between two Humes, 20.6 between two Tarutaru and 21.6 between two Galka. The color goes by the number you see, so between two Humes 20.8 is plain and 20.9 is colored. **10 yalms** is songs, Protectra, Shellra and the Circles. **8 yalms** is Phantom Roll, and **16 yalms** is Phantom Roll with Luzaf's Ring. Samples take the color as if both of you were Hume. A Bard with a string instrument can reach past 10 with songs, so **10 yalms** can warn early for them. The color goes by distance only, and a wall can still block a spell. The game's place for another player can trail them by a few tenths while they run, so right at the line the color is a close guide.

**Include me** can be **Always**, **Only in a party** or **Never**. **Only in a party** hides Party list while you're alone.

**Most per member** at **All that fit** keeps every effect that passes your filters. Any other number stops there. **Sort** offers **Longest remaining**, **Oldest first**, **Ending soonest**, **Name** and **My order**. Longest remaining is the default. It sorts **4h**, then **3m**, then **57s**, and effects without a countdown go last. **My order** goes by the list right under **Sort**, so every member's row has its effects in the same order. **Add to My order**, on the **Filters** tab, puts an effect on it and sets **Sort** to **My order**. On the list, **Up**, **Down** and **Remove** arrange it. Effects you didn't list follow the ones you did, by name. With nothing on the list, they go by name and a line says so. **Icons fill**, on **Size and place**, picks the starting edge. Pinned effects come first.

While party cooldowns show beside the party list, a line says so. **Change on Party cooldowns** opens that panel. With **None** picked and no party cooldowns there, the line says nothing shows. Party buffs also show in a list, on Party members' timers, and over heads, on Overhead icons. **Party buffs elsewhere**, at the end of the tab, has **Open Party members' timers** and **Open Overhead icons**.

An effect with no known duration keeps its icon, with no countdown and no mark. Level Sync always shows only its icon. Your own effect timers use the client's durations. A complete icon snapshot from the game tells Vantage which effects are on same-zone members of your own party, even outside entity range, but it gives no duration. Applications you observe can add estimated timers. An empty row doesn't prove a member has no buffs. Your own KO doesn't clear what Vantage saw on other members. In an alert's **Who to watch**, **My party, including me** selects your group and **Other alliance parties** selects the other two groups.

### Size and place

Beside the game's list, with **Icon size beside the game's list** on **Fill the row**, each member's icons fill the step from one row to the next, centered on the member's row. So one member's icons touch the next member's. Where the step isn't a whole number of pixels, as with some game menu sizes, Vantage rounds the icons up. One member's icons may then cover the next member's by 1 px, but they never leave a gap. There's no space between the icons either. A line at the top of the **Size and place** tab says how big that is in your game. At 1920x1080 with the game menu at its usual size, the rows are 14 px tall and 20 px apart, so the icons are 20 px. With no one else in the party, the step comes from the game's empty party slots, or from a party earlier this session at the same row height. Until one of them gives it, the icons are as tall as the row and the line gives no number.

**Icon size beside the game's list** picks a size of its own, from 12 to 128 px. A size smaller than the step sits in the middle of each member's row. A bigger one can't fit beside the rows, so each group's buffs become a stack beside the game's list instead. Each member gets a line with their name, then their icons at that size, in the order of the game's rows. The stack has no frame. Its last member's icons end level with the bottom of the game's list, so it grows up as people join. It stays on the **Buff side**, keeps the **Gap from the game's list**, and moves with the list, or where you drag it. **Member names** and **Short name length**, on **Look**, set the names. **Longest row**, the distance, party cooldowns and countdowns work as they do beside the rows, and **Countdown position** and **Countdown gap** work as they do in a panel of its own. In an alliance the stacks pile up in the order of the game's windows, 8 px apart. When they wouldn't all fit on the screen, they all take the biggest size that does, and when even a pixel over the rows wouldn't fit, the icons fill the rows again. Countdowns **Below icon** make each line taller, so they lower that size. The size you picked stays saved either way. The line at the top of the tab says what your size does, and when the stacks have no room on the screen.

With **Original FFXI** or **XI View** party buff icons, Vantage makes each icon from its 32 px picture at the size it draws, so it stays sharp. A new size takes a moment. Until it's made, Vantage draws the 32 px picture scaled. An icon set of your own works the same way, from each picture as it is, at sizes up to 128 px.

**Longest row** sets how wide a row can get. A group's row grows with its buffs up to that width, and it always has room for one whole icon. With the distance on, its column is added to that. Effects that don't fit are left out after the last one that does, with no count.

**Icon size in a panel of its own** shows while a group that's showing is in a panel of its own. It sets how big the icons are there. A skin and a **Size** button keep it. **Size** doesn't change the size beside the game's rows, and neither does a skin. A smaller menu resolution in the game's own settings makes the game's rows, and the step, bigger.

While **Countdowns** is on, **Countdown size** shows too. **Countdown position** joins it while a group that's showing is in a panel of its own or a stack. **Countdown gap** joins them while **Countdown position** is **Below icon**. The countdowns use **Font** on the main page. Beside the game's rows, the time sits inside the icon. A panel of its own and a stack use the position and gap you pick.

**Buff side** puts each group on the left or right of its game window, including a group you dragged. **Gap from the game's list** is the space between the game's party list and the nearest icon, or the distance while it's on. It starts at 26 px. **Icons fill** fills each row **Left to right** or **Right to left**, and you can still change it while Locked. Beside the game's list, a row stays next to the game's row either way.

Dragging never puts a group in a panel of its own. **Put moved groups back** puts every group you moved back beside the game's list on the **Buff side**. If there's no room on that side, such as with a list at the screen's edge, the buffs don't show and a line on this tab says so.

The **Show** tab has a switch for each group that shows, under **Groups**: **Your party in a panel of its own**, **Alliance 1 in a panel of its own** and **Alliance 2 in a panel of its own**. Each one puts that group's buffs in a framed panel where the group is drawn now, and keeps the panel's edge next to the game's list. The panel is up to **Longest row** wide and as tall as its members' buffs need. It moves up if the screen has no room under it. In an alliance, a panel that would cover another group goes beside that group, away from the game's list. If there's no room beside it, the panel goes above it. With no room for either, the panels beside the list share the screen's height. They work while Locked. Turn one off to put that group back beside the game's rows. A panel has its own position and size. Member labels and effects wrap inside it, and scrolling reaches the rest. In Arrange on screen it doesn't scroll, so a click on it picks it like any other display. **Panels of their own**, on this tab, shows while a group is in one, and it starts open. In it, **Panel to edit** picks one, with **Panel width**, **Panel height**, **Across the screen** and **Down the screen**. Or unlock, hold Shift and drag the panel, or drag an edge or corner to resize it. None of these resize the game's windows.

While a group that's showing is in a panel of its own, a line under the panel's top line names it, such as **Your party is in a panel of its own.** Under that line is **Put party buffs back beside the party list**. It puts every group in a panel of its own back beside the game's rows as one Undo step, and it works while Locked. Each group keeps where you moved it, and each panel keeps its size and place for next time. **Snap back beside the game's list when released**, in **Panels of their own**, puts a panel back beside its group's game window when you let go of it there.

**Member order** shows then too, and it reorders a panel. Pick a group under **Group to order** and move its slots with **Up** and **Down**. The party itself doesn't change. **Reset this tab** puts every group's slots and spot back. Each panel of its own gets the size and place turning it on gives, where it is now, so no two panels cover each other.

A group needs the client's row positions. If Vantage can't read a group's rows, it leaves that group undrawn instead of drawing it at a guessed spot. A member whose row isn't in the game's list yet, as just after a join, gets no icons while the others keep theirs. The **Size and place** tab and `/vg report` say why.

## Overhead icons

Vantage can draw buffs and debuffs above heads in the game world. Overhead icons start off. Turn them on with **Overhead icons** on the main page or with `/vg overhead on`. **Edit** on that row opens the panel, and **On** at the top of the panel turns them on too. Nothing over a head can be clicked, so there's no lock. The icons sit under every Vantage panel, and the game gets the mouse as usual.

A block shows only what Vantage has seen. The game sends an action only to players within 30 yalms of whoever acts, and it sends a wear-off message only to whoever cast the effect. So debuffs that others put on a mob run on an estimate. What a weapon skill or a pet's move puts on a mob is taken from what the action did, since the game says nothing of it (see **Hits on a mob**). If one of those runs out with no wear-off, it keeps its icon with no countdown. The game sends buff icons for your own party only, so blocks over the alliance and other players hold what Vantage saw land on them.

**Show icons over**, at the top of the **Show** tab, has a switch for each kind of unit: **Your target**, **Your subtarget**, **Mobs your party claimed**, **Watched mobs**, **Mobs your party debuffed**, **Your party**, **Your alliance**, **Other players**, **Your pet**, **Other players' pets** and **You**. Other players, their pets and you start off. While **Watched mobs** is ticked and nobody is watched, a line says so and has **Watch my target**. It adds your target to Watched mobs and to the list on Watched enemy timers, and turns that panel on. With its **When empty** on **Hide**, as at first, that panel shows once it has a timer to show. **Most units at once** keeps the most important ones, nearest first. It's 10 at first. A unit whose effects your hide rules all cover has nothing to draw, so it takes none of those places. **Farthest away** sets how far blocks go. It's 30 yalms at first and 50 at most. **Fade starts at** sets where they start to fade, and it's 20 at first. Blocks fade down to **Faded opacity** at the limit. Your target never fades. **Only your target and party in towns** keeps towns quiet. Sealion's Den isn't counted as a town. Each block stays fixed on its own unit. Where two overlap, Vantage draws the nearer one on top, and your target's is always on top.

A mob the game keeps out of sight never gets a block, even as your target. That covers an invisible one, a worm gone under the ground and an antlion buried in wait. When you load or reload Vantage, a mob that's already in view gets its block once the game next updates it, as when it moves.

**Change the settings for**, in a box under the top line, picks the kind of unit that the rows below it change. It shows on every tab. Where a tab's rows for the kind start, a line names it, such as `For Mobs, as picked above:`. Your target, your subtarget and the claimed, watched and debuffed mobs share the Mobs kind. A player you target uses their own kind. For **Layout**, **Single bar** puts debuffs, then buffs, in one row of 8 on mobs and the party. **Two lines** puts debuffs nearest the name and buffs above, or the other way around with **Swap the lines**. **Split** puts buffs on the left and debuffs on the right, or the other way around with **Swap the sides**. Each has its own limits, either **Icons shown** or **Debuffs shown** and **Buffs shown**. Vantage leaves out icons past a limit, the least important and newest first. Nothing marks that any were left out. In Split, mobs have **Crowd control icon on its own**. It draws Sleep, Silence, Petrification, Stun, Charm, Amnesia, Terror, Lullaby or Doom larger, next to the middle. Each kind also has its own **Countdowns**, and its own **Show** with **Buffs and debuffs**, **Buffs only** or **Debuffs only**.

For mobs, **Light up buffs you can remove** lights a mob's buff in every layout when your job and level can take it off now. Dispel works from RDM 32, or /RDM with a main job of 64 and up. Magic Finale works from BRD 33, or /BRD at 66 and up. Blank Gaze works from BLU 38 while it's set. The game sends your blue magic set when you zone in and when you change it, so after `/addon reload vantage` Blank Gaze counts once you zone or change your set. Dark Shot works from COR 40 with a Dark Card or Trump Card, a Marksmanship weapon (gun or crossbow) and its ammo. For Party, Alliance and You, **Light up debuffs you can remove** lights a debuff the same way (see **Debuffs you can remove**). It starts on. Erase reaches your party only, so on an alliance member a debuff that only Erase removes stays unlit. A lit debuff keeps its place among the icons.

The **Filters** tab goes by the kind picked too. It has **Use the Hide list**, with **Edit the list** to open it, and **Only effects I cast**, **Only buffs I cast** and **Only debuffs from my party**. The last two are grey while **Only effects I cast** is on, and each is gone while **Show** leaves out the side it's for. It also has **Effect**, which reads **Choose an effect** until you pick one, with **Show only** and **Hide here** for single effects. Those effects list by name under them. **Reset this tab** puts the filters of the kind picked back, those two lists too. At first, mobs show your party's debuffs and their own buffs, and the alliance shows its debuffs. Your own block shows everything you have on. Everyone else shows their debuffs and the buffs you cast.

**Show these effects:** lists groups of effects for the kind: **Corsair rolls**, **Bard songs**, **Protect and Shell**, **Food**, **Barspells**, **Stances** and **Signet and similar**. That last group holds Signet, Sanction, Level Sync, Level Restriction, Reraise, Mounted and Battlefield. A ticked group shows and an unticked group hides, and the other filters still apply. An effect on the **Show only** list shows either way. Bust and the debuff songs aren't in a group, so they stay. Other players, party members, the alliance and pets start with Protect and Shell, Food and Signet and similar off. Mobs and you start with everything on. The list hides while **Show** is **Debuffs only**. What you ticked stays, and it decides nothing until the list is back.

**Always show** has **Copy Image** and **Third Eye**. A ticked effect shows whoever cast it, even with **Only effects I cast**, **Only buffs I cast** or **Debuffs only** on. It also goes ahead of the other buffs, so a buff limit doesn't cut it. On a player the debuffs still go first, so a **Single bar** full of debuffs can leave it out. An unticked effect doesn't show unless it's on **Show only**, where the other filters decide as they do for any effect. **Hide here** and the **Hide list** still hide it. While **Show only** has effects on it, only those show. Mobs and you start ticked, and your party and the alliance start unticked. On a mob or an alliance member, Vantage follows Copy Image and Third Eye itself (see **Shadows and Third Eye**). Other players and pets don't have this line, because their icon wouldn't keep up.

Icons follow how big a unit is on screen, going by its distance from the camera and its size from Phoenix hitboxes. So a dragon's icons are larger than a goblin's. The **Size and place** tab holds **Icon size**, **Smallest**, **Largest**, **Bigger on your target**, **Crowd control icon size** and **Countdowns go inside icons under**. **Shrink with distance** and **Bigger units get bigger icons** are in the **Fine-tune scaling** fold, with a line that says how many pixels the icons come to at a few distances. Those apply to every kind, and **Size** on the main page scales the icons too. **Icon size for this kind** comes after them, for the kind picked under **Change the settings for**. The same tab has **Where the icons sit**, which puts them above the name, or under the head for players who turn names off. It also has **Gap above the name** and the spaces between icons, lines and the two sides.

When icons sit above the name, Vantage reads where the game draws each name straight from the client every frame, and the target cursor over your target too. It puts each block **Gap above the name** over the name, over an icon in front of the name, or over the cursor when it's higher, near or far. That gap is 3 pixels at first. If the client can't be read that way, say after a client update, Vantage falls back to guessing. It also guesses for one unit that fails a check. The guess uses the head height, how far the middle of the name sits over the head, and how big Vantage takes the name and cursor to be. That distance is half a yalm for players at first, and none for mobs and pets. The sizes it takes for the name and cursor shrink with distance and stop growing up close. Those values, and the race heights, only matter for the guess, so they aren't on the panel. `/vg set` changes them. For the name, use `overhead.types.party.name_lift`, `overhead.layout.name_height` and `overhead.layout.name_cap`. The first of those is the party's, and each type has its own. For the cursor, use `overhead.layout.target_height` and `overhead.layout.target_cap`. `/vg report` says which way each unit's block was placed and why.

The **Look** tab starts with a line saying its icons are the main page's **Icons**, as Overhead icons have no icon choice of their own. **Open Icons** beside it opens **Artwork** on the main page, where **Icons** is. Then **Look** has **Countdown color**. Bold, shadow and outline are under **Text effects** on the main page, and the expiry blink is under **While playing**. An effect with no known time shows its icon with no countdown.

**Lit icons**, a fold on **Look**, shows while a light is on. It has **Glow around lit icons** and **Light color**, and they apply to every kind. **Lit icons**, under **Colors** on the main page, sets them on every display at once.

**If icons sit too high or too low**, on **Size and place**, starts closed. For the kind picked above, it has **Head height offset** in yalms, and **Across** and **Down** in pixels. Under them, **Add head offset** raises or lowers the block over one unit, picked by its exact name. **Reset this tab** there clears every unit's offset. Type the name in **Unit name**, or fill it in with **Use my target's name**. `/vg overhead calibrate` prints your target's head height and where it comes from. The race heights, the hitbox line for mobs and the camera checks aren't on the panel. `/vg set` changes them by their path under `overhead`, such as `/vg set overhead.head.race_heights.8 2.2` for Galka.

Vantage reads the game's camera at the 2nd BeginScene of each frame, where the blocks hold still. If `/vg overhead calibrate` says the camera isn't in use because the frame had too few scenes, try `/vg set overhead.camera.capture present`. That reads the camera when the frame is shown. `/vg set overhead.camera.capture scene` goes back.

**Show samples** at the top of the panel, **Show samples** on the main page and `/vg overhead preview` show one block of sample icons above your name, where your own block would sit. It's laid out as the kind picked under **Change the settings for**. When you aren't on screen, it shows a third of the way down the middle of the screen instead. The samples draw over the settings window, and clicks go through them. They create no tracking or alert events. While the icons are off, the panel's **Show samples** says to turn them on first and changes nothing. **When to show** choices that hide the blocks leave the samples showing, and `/vg report` says the conditions hide them.

Blocks hide in cutscenes and events, on the map, while the game's interface is hidden and while zoning. A unit's block also hides while the unit is dead, not drawn or behind the camera. Overhead changes support profiles, shared presentation, Undo, Redo, **Reset all of this display** and `/vg reset overhead`. Picking a skin sets their countdown and light colors back to how they started, and has them follow **Font** on the main page. Their switches, layouts and filters stay.

## Party cooldowns

When Vantage sees a party or alliance member use a two-hour, a key job ability or Utsusemi, it counts down its recast for them. **Edit** on the main page's **Party cooldowns** row opens the panel. **On**, at the top of it, is the watch. The watch starts off. If you turn it on while the estimates have nowhere to show, the list turns on too. While the watch is off, Vantage counts nothing and keeps nothing, and the three places below show as off. Ticking one of them, there or on the main page's row, turns the watch on, with the times in that place alone. Unticking the last one turns the watch off.

- It watches every two-hour of the jobs in era, and each of those is 7200 seconds on Phoenix. It also watches Provoke, Berserk, Warcry, Divine Seal, Martyr, Devotion, Elemental Seal, Convert, Sneak Attack, Trick Attack, Sentinel, Rampart, Fealty, Chivalry, Call Beast, Nightingale, Troubadour, Meditate, Call Wyvern, Random Deal and Activate, with Phoenix's era recasts, and the spells Utsusemi: Ichi and Utsusemi: Ni. Untick one under **Abilities and spells to watch**, a fold on the **Filters** tab, to stop counting it, and **Watch every ability and spell** ticks them all again. Its title says how many are ticked.
- An ability's time is an estimate from its base recast. Merits can make some of them end sooner, and the tooltip says which. A THF can merit Sneak Attack and Trick Attack down to 50 seconds, and a /THF's are always 60. A spell's time isn't an estimate. It's the recast the game sent with the cast, with the caster's haste and Fast Cast already in it, so Utsusemi: Ni from a NIN with haste can be well under its 45 seconds. It comes in whole seconds, so it can end up to a second before the spell is ready. A cast stopped by Paralysis or Intimidate doesn't count, as the game puts no recast on it. The game sends an action only to players within 30 yalms of whoever acts, so Vantage doesn't see a use farther off. Vantage doesn't count pets' moves. It doesn't count your own either, because your recast panels read those from the client.
- A Wild Card that lands on a member clears their job abilities, but not their spells. On a 5 or 6 it clears their two-hour too, unless their main job is COR. A Random Deal can clear one of a member's running job abilities, but the game doesn't say which, so Vantage keeps counting it until their next use. A member's Dismiss with their wyvern at full HP clears their Call Wyvern, and a Deactivate with their automaton at full HP clears their Activate. Vantage reads the pet's HP from the game, and when it can't, the estimate stays. A job change clears all but the two-hour and the spells. A support job the game hides for a while isn't a job change. Vantage drops a member who leaves the party.

The times show in three places, under **Show them** on the **Show** tab:

- **Beside the party list** starts on, so Party list shows the times once the watch is on, if Party list is on with a group ticked. If it isn't, turning the watch on unticks Beside the party list. Each is the ability's or spell's icon with its time left. They go first on the member's row beside the game's party list, nearest the game's window, after the distance while that's on, and before their buffs and debuffs. They draw on **Party list**. Ticking this turns Party list on too, with only the times there if it was off. If Party list is off, or no group is ticked there, the **Show** tab says so and has a button to put it right. To show them with no buffs or debuffs, pick **None** on the **Show** tab of Party list.
- **Over heads** starts off. It puts a line of up to six on the member's overhead block. A member with a time counting gets a block for it, even when their kind's filters leave none of their effects. They draw on **Overhead icons**. Ticking this while Overhead icons shows no one in your party ticks **Your party** and **Your alliance** there, and turns it on, with only them, if it was off. If Overhead icons is off, or **Your party** and **Your alliance** are both unticked there, the **Show** tab says so and has a button to put it right.
- **In a list** starts off. It's a display with one row for each, with its member, ability or spell and time left, in the timer panels' font and colors. **Show its heading** shows the list's title. **Sort** puts the ready soonest first, or sorts by member. The job ticks on **Filters** show only the jobs you tick. **Most shown** and the list's height cut rows past them, with nothing after the last one. You set the height with **Height** on **Size and place**, where **Fit** fits the rows and any other value is a fixed height. The list starts locked. Turn off **Locked**, then hold Shift and drag it to move it, or drag an edge to resize it. While the watch and the list are on, **Show samples** shows sample rows that follow your **Sort**, job ticks and **Abilities and spells to watch**. While they aren't, it says to turn them on first. Like the alert notices, the list doesn't pause with the timer panels or follow **Hide timer panels while I type**. **Locked**, the job ticks and the **Size and place** and **When to show** tabs belong to the list, so they go away while it's off. **Abilities and spells to watch** stays on **Filters**, because every place counts by it.

While the watch is on, Party list's samples (`/vg party preview`) give their first five sample members a sample estimate each. A party or alliance block among the overhead samples gets a line of them too. That way each place shows in its normal look. With the watch off, they show none. Those samples belong to Party list and the overhead icons, and they turn those on, not the watch.

`/vg reset party_cooldowns` or **Reset all of this display** restores all of it, the watch as well as the list.

## Phantom Roll

While you or a party member rolls, the Phantom Roll display shows the roll. It starts off. Turn it on with **Phantom Roll** on the main page. **Edit** on that row opens the panel, and **On** at the top of the panel turns it on too.

- Each roll shows its icon, its name and its total. The total is in the lucky color on its lucky number and the unlucky color on its unlucky number. At the right is the time left to **Double-Up**. That's 45 seconds from the Phantom Roll, and a Double-Up doesn't start it again. Vantage times yours by your Double-Up Chance and estimates another Corsair's from their roll. Another Corsair's ends when their Double-Up Chance goes, as after a Fold. After a bust it shows **Bust** and the bust's time left, until the bust wears off. The bust keeps its own line when the Corsair rolls the same roll again, and two busts each show their own time.
- **Show what it gives** shows what the roll gives you at its total, from Phoenix's era roll tables, or what a bust takes from the Corsair. Vantage adds the job bonus only for a main job it sees in your party, on a total of 11 or less. Gear can also add it at random, and Vantage can't see that. When the Corsair's Corsair level is below your level, Vantage lowers the value the way Phoenix lowers it.
- **Show lucky and unlucky numbers** adds a line with each roll's two numbers. The total takes the lucky and unlucky colors either way.
- **Show the bust chance** shows the chance that one more Double-Up busts, based on the total. A Double-Up adds 1 to 6, and 12 or more busts. So a 6 busts 1 time in 6, and an 11 always does. With Snake Eye on the Corsair it adds 1, so only 11 busts.
- **Show who got it** shows your party in party order. Vantage dims members the roll didn't reach and those who already have the roll from another Corsair. After a bust, it also dims those who lost it. A roll reaches party members within 8 yalms of the Corsair, or 16 with Luzaf's Ring. The game sends an action only to players within 30 yalms of whoever acts, so Vantage doesn't see a roll farther off.
- It shows the numbers only and never says whether to Double-Up.
- The roll tables, what each roll gives and Snake Eye adding 1 are Phoenix's era rules, which the server plays with its content restriction (RESTRICT_CONTENT) on. Vantage assumes it is on.

Rolls go newest first. **Most rolls shown** and the display's height cut rolls past them, with nothing after the last one. You set the height with **Height** on **Size and place**, where **Fit** fits the rows and any other value is a fixed height. Members past the display's width are left out the same way. The display starts locked. Turn off **Locked**, then hold Shift and drag it to move it, or drag an edge to resize it. **Show samples** shows two sample rolls while the display is on, and says to turn it on first while it isn't. Its colors and **Show its heading** are on **Show**. It has no **Look** or **Filters** tab. Like the party cooldown list, it doesn't pause with the timer panels or follow **Hide timer panels while I type**. `/vg reset rolls` or **Reset all of this display** restores it.

## Pet

While you have a pet, the pet display shows it. It starts off. Turn it on with **Pet** on the main page, and **Edit** on that row opens its panel.

- It shows the pet's name, and with **Show HP, MP and TP** bars for its HP, MP and TP as the client has them. Their colors, **HP bar**, **MP bar** and **TP bar**, are on one line right under that tick. MP shows once the game gives the pet some, because a pet with no MP reads 0. A pet too far off for the client to show stays on the display with its effects, and the game keeps its HP up to date. If there's no word of it for 90 minutes, it leaves.
- **Replace the game's pet bar**, under **While playing** on the main page, starts off. With it on, the pet's name, HP bar and TP that the game draws above the party list are hidden, so this display is the only one. It needs the display on with **Show HP, MP and TP**. Whenever Vantage can't show your pet there, the game draws its own again: while the sample pet shows, after `/vg hide`, while Ashita's `/hideui` is on, while its **When to show** hides it, and if Vantage's displays stop drawing because of an error. While the display or **Show HP, MP and TP** is off, or the sample pet shows, a line under the tick says why. After `/vg hide`, a line at the top of the main page says the displays are hidden. An error that stops the displays is reported in chat. When your own rules hide the Pet display, such as on the map, the game's bar stays hidden with it. While its **When to show** hides it, the game draws its own pet bar again, except in **Arrange on screen**. To do this, Vantage changes one spot in the game's memory, so the game lays the party list out as it does with no pet, and puts it back when you untick this or unload Vantage. Before it changes anything, it checks that this part of the game is exactly the one it knows, and if a game update or something else in the game has changed it, the game keeps its own pet bar and the line says so. Vantage still sends nothing. The setting is each character's own, and profiles, exports and shared layouts don't carry it.
- **Effects** can be **Buffs and debuffs**, **Buffs only**, **Debuffs only** or **None**. It shows the effects Vantage saw land on the pet, debuffs first, with their countdowns while **Show effect countdowns** is on. **Show effects as** draws them as **Icons**, which wrap onto more rows when they don't fit the width, or as a **List with names**. The list has one line for each effect, with its icon, its name and its time left over a fill of how much is left. An effect near its end blinks, as **Blink before an effect ends** sets for every display. The **Filters** tab has the display's own **Show only** and **Hide here**, and **Use the Hide list**. The Pets group in that list matches your pet.
- **Show pet command recasts** shows your recasts of BST's Sic, Reward, Call Beast, Charm, Fight, Heel, Leave and Stay, SMN's Blood Pact: Rage and Ward, Assault, Retreat and Release, DRG's Call Wyvern, Spirit Link, Dismiss and Deep Breathing, and PUP's Activate, Repair, Deploy, Deactivate, Retrieve, Maneuvers, Role Reversal and Ventriloquy. On those jobs it also shows your main job's two-hour. The soonest ready go first. The time is the client's own. A fill shows how much of the full recast is left. The full recast is the one Vantage saw start, or else Phoenix's era recast less your merits on that main job, such as Sic's 120 seconds less 4 for each merit and Repair's 180 less 3. The game sends your merits as you zone. So after `/addon reload vantage`, the fill of a timer that's already running counts no merits until your next zone. Ready isn't in this era, so its timer is Sic alone. Repair's flat 180 and Ready staying out are Phoenix's era rules, which the server plays with its content restriction (RESTRICT_CONTENT) on, as Vantage assumes.

The **Show** tab has more ticks under four headings. Under **More about any pet** and **Avatar and wyvern** every one starts off. Under **Automaton** only the maneuvers start on, and under **Charmed and jug pets** only the charm and jug time does. Each of the others adds a line to the display, or a number or a mark on a bar. A line has its name on the left and its value or a countdown on the right, and it's in the warning color when it wants your attention. The lines sit under the maneuvers and above the recasts. A value too long for the display is cut at its right, so widen the display to read all of it. With **Show details when I point at a timer** on, pointing at a line says where it comes from. **Show samples** shows a made-up line for each tick that fits your job, or your support job when your main job has no pet.

Under **More about any pet**:

- **Show what my pet is fighting** adds **Fighting**, with the mob's name and its HP percent. It's in the warning color when that mob isn't your target. The game names the mob only while your pet is engaged.
- **Show what my pet is doing** adds **Casting** or **Readies** with the spell or move your pet has started. Once it finishes, **Last move** names it for a minute, with its damage on the first target when it did any. The game sends an action only to players within 30 yalms of whoever acts, so Vantage misses what a pet farther off does.
- **Show the distance to my pet** adds **Distance** in yalms. It's in the warning color past 25 yalms as a BST with a jug pet or a charmed mob out, which is as far as Fight reaches, past 20 as a PUP with your automaton out, for Repair, and past 14 as a DRG with your wyvern out, for Healing Breath. Any other pet has no such limit, such as a BST's avatar or a PUP's jug pet, as Fight, Repair and Healing Breath are each for those pets alone. A pet too far off for the client to show reads **Out of view**.
- **Show how long my pet has been out** adds **Out for**, counted from when Vantage first saw the pet in this zone. A zone change or `/addon reload vantage` starts it again.

Under **Automaton**, for a PUP, main or support:

- **Show maneuvers and overload risk** shows a line for each element with a maneuver up, when you're a PUP, main or support. Each line has its icon, its name, a square for each maneuver, the time until the next of them ends, and the overload risk your last maneuver of that element reported. The risk then falls by 1 every 3 seconds, which is the slowest the server lowers it. So the real risk is at most what's shown.
- **Show automaton HP as a number** starts off. With it on, when you're a PUP, main or support, your automaton's HP bar shows its HP and max HP, like 812/1040, instead of a percent. Only automatons get this, because the game sends every other pet's HP as a percent. The number comes from the automaton status the server sends you, and Vantage shows it only while it agrees with the HP percent the game shows. Until the server sends it, like right after `/addon reload vantage`, and whenever the two don't agree, like at 0 HP, the bar shows the percent. With **Show details when I point at a timer** on, pointing at the number says where it comes from. The row shows while **Show HP, MP and TP** is on.
- **Show attachment timers** adds a line for each attachment on your automaton that fires on its own: Strobe, Reactive Shield, Heat Capacitor, Replicator, Barrage Turbine, Shock Absorber, Flashbulb, Eraser, Mana Converter, Economizer, Disruptor and Regulator. A line counts down to when the attachment can fire again. After that it reads **Ready** while a maneuver of its element is up, or it names the maneuver it needs, such as **Needs Earth**, in the warning color. The recasts are Phoenix's: 30 seconds for Strobe and Eraser, 45 for Flashbulb, 60 for Reactive Shield, Replicator, Disruptor and Regulator, 90 for Heat Capacitor, and 180 for Barrage Turbine, Shock Absorber, Mana Converter and Economizer. Every one starts its recast when the automaton comes out, and Economizer starts with 60 seconds. A zone change starts them all again, because Phoenix makes the automaton anew in each zone. An attachment fires only while the automaton fights, and some wait for more, such as Replicator for half HP. The tooltip says what each one waits for. A II or III shares the line of the first, as it shares its recast. If Vantage loads with the automaton already out, the automaton's lines come back when it next moves or its HP changes, and an attachment's line reads **Not known yet** until that attachment fires or its recast has passed.
- **Show automaton MP as a number** makes the MP bar read the automaton's MP and max MP, like 168/420, instead of a percent. It comes from the same automaton status as the HP number, and it shows only while it agrees with the percent the game shows. The row shows while **Show HP, MP and TP** is on.
- **Show the automaton's own cooldowns** adds what the automaton does by itself. A Harlequin or Stormwaker frame casts, so it gets **Next spell** and a line for each kind of spell its head casts: **Healing**, **Enfeebling**, **Elemental** and **Status removal**. A Sharpshot frame gets **Ranged attack** and a Valoredge frame gets **Shield Bash**. Each line counts from when Vantage saw the automaton start its last one, reads **Ready** after that and **Not seen yet** before the first. A Spiritreaver head's Drain and Aspir count as enfeebling spells, as Phoenix counts them. The times are Phoenix's era numbers for your head and frame, with what Mana Booster, Mana Channeler, Damage Gauge, Drum Magazine and Barrier Module change for the maneuvers you have up. Optic Fiber's part in a Mana Channeler isn't counted. They're estimates, because the automaton acts on a tick of its own of up to 3 seconds.
- **Show the automaton's build** adds **Build** with its head and frame, then its Melee, Ranged and Magic skill with their caps, its seven stats with their bonuses, and its attachments, two to a line. It's all what the server last sent you as a PUP.

Under **Charmed and jug pets**, for a BST, main or support:

- **Show charm and jug time** gives an estimate of the time left for a pet you charmed. It comes from Phoenix's charm formula, with your CHR, your main level, the mob's level and the charm time gear you had on. The mob's level is the one a /check before the charm gave. Without one, the charm shows with no countdown. Familiar adds 25 to 30 minutes once, and the estimate counts 25. For a jug pet, **Leaves in** shows Phoenix's time for that pet, counted from your Call Beast. It's in the warning color for its last minute. Familiar doesn't change it. A zone change keeps the clock only if the same jug comes back within 30 seconds, and a jug that was already out when Vantage loaded has no clock. The display shows a pet for as long as the game does, through a zone change too. `/addon reload` keeps a jug's clock and a charm's estimate for as long as the game runs, without writing anything to disk.
- **Mark where Sic is ready** draws a mark on the TP bar at 1000 TP when you're a BST, main or support, with a jug pet or a charmed mob out. That's where Sic makes your pet use a move. An avatar, a wyvern or an automaton gets no mark, as Sic can't command them. Where the TP number reaches that far, the mark is a notch above the number and one below it. The row shows while **Show HP, MP and TP** is on.
- **Show what Reward would heal** adds **Reward** when you're a BST, main or support, with a jug pet or a charmed mob out, such as `Heals 412`. It goes by the pet food in your ammo slot, your MND and the gear you have on that adds to Reward, by Phoenix's era formula. Reward never heals more than your pet has lost. With no pet food in the slot, it reads **No pet food** in the warning color. Reward can't heal an avatar, a wyvern or an automaton, so with one of those out there is no Reward line, not even **No pet food**.

Under **Avatar and wyvern**:

- **Show what my avatar costs** adds **MP cost** when you're a SMN, main or support, with an avatar or a spirit out. Another pet, such as a wyvern, a jug pet or an automaton, has no such line. Vantage watches your own MP go down every 3 seconds with an avatar out, so the number already has your Refresh taken off. It reads like `7 a tick, 4:20 left`, where the time is how long your MP lasts at that rate, and it's in the warning color for the last minute. It reads **Measuring** until Vantage has seen two steps alike, and **None** once your MP hasn't gone down for 12 seconds. A drop of 30 MP or more counts as a spell or a pact, not as the avatar's cost.
- **Show when Healing Breath would fire** adds **Breath** when you're a DRG main with your wyvern out. Another pet, such as an avatar, a jug pet or a charmed mob, gets no Breath line, as only the wyvern uses Healing Breath. A wyvern uses Healing Breath when a spell of yours goes off and you're at a third of your HP or less with WHM, BLM, RDM, SMN or BLU as your support job, or at a quarter or less with PLD, DRK, BRD or NIN. A Drachen Armet makes those a half and a third. You also have to be within 14 yalms of the wyvern. The line then reads **Ready for you**. It goes by your HP and max HP, as Phoenix does, so it can still wait while the game shows you right at 33%. It reads **Ready for** and a party member's name when your support job lets the wyvern heal the party and a member within 14 yalms of it is that low. It reads **Too far** in the warning color when you're low enough but more than 14 yalms from it. Otherwise it reads the HP it waits for, such as **At 33% HP**, or **Not with this sub job**.

Your pet's alerts are on **Alerts**, under **Your pet** at the end of its **Show** tab. **Pet alerts**, at the end of Pet's **Show** tab, has **Open Alerts** to get there. They all start off.

- **Tell me when my pet's HP is low** shows a notice when your pet's HP falls to **Pet HP is low at** or under it. That's 25 percent at first.
- **Tell me when my pet has 1000 TP** shows one when its TP reaches 1000.
- **Tell me when a charm or a jug pet is about to end** shows one when a charm's estimate or a jug pet's time has a minute left.

A pet notice shows where alert notices do, for 5 seconds, with no sound and no chat line. Each one fires once as its line is crossed, and again only after the pet has been back on the other side. A pet that comes out already past a line raises nothing. The notices need the Pet display on, and **Alerts** on and not muted. Alerts says so while it's muted, or while the Pet display is off, with **Turn on the Pet display**.

The game sends no list of a pet's effects. When an effect on a pet ends, the game tells only the player it came from, so only effects from you end exactly. These have a length Vantage knows, and they leave at your wear-off or on time:

- Reward and Repair's Regen, Deep Breathing's Magic Atk. Boost on your wyvern, and Overdrive and overload on your automaton, which take the time of your own.
- What Empathy copies with Spirit Link, each with the time yours had left. When you have more of them than ranks, the server picks at random, so then none shows.
- Your avatar's wards, with the time of your own copy from that ward, or else the length from your summoning skill and level. With neither, there's no countdown.

Phoenix checks for expiry every 0.4 seconds, so the wear-off for one of these can come just after it leaves on time. That wear-off still counts for **Effect removed / wore off** alerts.

A level restriction or Level Sync takes every effect off a pet you called, and the game says nothing about it. So Vantage clears them when your level changes with one of those. A mob you charmed keeps its own.

What Reward, Repair and Spirit Link cleanse leaves when you use them. A hit wakes your pet, as it wakes a mob, ends its Stoneskin and takes the time off Bind, but a mob's Nightmare stays. Your pet's own next swing, spell or move takes Sleep, Petrification, Stun and Terror off it, Nightmare too (see **Hits on a mob**). So does your pet walking a yalm or more, as Phoenix holds a pet under any of them still. A mob's spikes end its Stoneskin too. The rest are estimates. One whose estimate ran out shows with no countdown and leaves as other estimates do.

What your pet does counts as yours. **Only effects I cast** and **Only buffs I cast** keep it on the timer panels and overhead icons. **Also list enemies I debuff** lists the mobs it debuffed, and the effect's tooltip says it came from your pet. Learned times still come from your own casts alone.

**Most effects shown**, **Most recasts shown**, the display's width and its height cut what's past them, with nothing after the last one. It starts locked. Turn off **Locked**, then hold Shift and drag it. **Show samples** shows a sample pet of your job. It has no **Look** tab, as its bar colors are on **Show**. Like the party cooldown list, it doesn't pause with the timer panels or follow **Hide timer panels while I type**. `/vg reset pet` or **Reset all of this display** puts it back as it started, but **Replace the game's pet bar** and the pet alerts on **Alerts** stay as they are.

## Recasts

Open the Recasts panel with **Edit** on the Recasts row of the main page, or type `/vg recasts`. **Show as**, on its **Show** tab, chooses **A panel for each kind** or **One panel for all**. A panel for each kind is the default. It has separate spell, ability and equipped enchanted-item panels, and they start off. Tick the ones you want on the main page, or type `/vg recasts show` to turn on all three. **Editing** picks which of the three the tabs change.

An ability's row has the ability's name. When two abilities share a recast, the row names both and ends in **(shared)**. A recast that a whole group of abilities shares goes by the group's name: **Maneuvers**, **Phantom Roll**, **Quick Draw**, **Sic**, **Blood Pact: Rage** and **Blood Pact: Ward**. The game keeps one timer for the group, so the row can't say which of them you used. For a PUP, **Show maneuvers and overload risk** on the Pet display shows which maneuvers are up.

Choose **One panel for all** or type `/vg recasts combine` to put your cooldowns in one panel. The ticks on the main page choose which spell, ability and item rows it includes. The one panel has its own filters, sorting, pinning, labels, fields, look and placement. Those settings don't change the three separate panels. Type `/vg recasts separate` to go back to their saved layout.

`/vg recasts show` and `/vg recasts hide` work on whichever way is in use. Hiding or switching keeps the other layout and the alert rules. The one panel starts on when you choose it, and **On** can turn it off.

Recast panels start as thin rails, with a dark area behind their text. Cooldown settings use **Ready soonest**, and **Ready soon** on **Look** colors a recast near the end. **Reset all of this display** restores only that panel. It leaves the way recasts show, the artwork, the recast tints and the ready pulse as they are, and so does **Reset this tab** on **Show**.

Each panel has the same controls as status timers: bars, expanded bars, icons and text layouts, row parts, colors, filters, custom labels, order, groups, pinning, moving with something and placement. Shift drag and resize a panel to wrap its contents. **Text** shows one row for each recast, with its name and countdown and no icon or bar. You can't show or hide its parts. Labels, dividers and colors still apply, and the parts you show stay saved for the bar layouts.

**Icons show** picks **The effect it gives** or **The spell or ability**, and the effect it gives is the default. With the effect it gives, a spell or ability that gives a buff or debuff, such as Protect, Paralyze, Dia, a song, Sneak or Berserk, shows that status icon in your **Icons** artwork. A few job abilities show theirs only once you've used them this session. Actions with no status, shared timers that could be several actions, and items keep their own picture. So do an action that gives its status only sometimes, such as Jump, and a status whose picture is missing.

Otherwise, recast rows use the client's spell, ability and item pictures. Spell and ability icons keep their native tier markings and component colors. Choose **Recast icons**, under **Artwork** on the main page, for **Original FFXI** or **XI View** action artwork. Missing XI View artwork falls back to stock. Item pictures come from the client resource manager whatever you choose there. Artwork stays in your installed client, and Vantage doesn't bundle any game images. Five ninjutsu spells use an approximate icon shadow, because the addon renderer can't do their native subtractive blend. Their foreground artwork still comes from the client.

Shared ability timers use the actions known right now, when the client gives that information. If several actions have different pictures, the icon group shows up to four different pictures. Hover it for details that name the shared timer. A recast with no client artwork keeps an empty icon box. It never borrows an unrelated status icon. Which icon shows doesn't change a cooldown's length or how Vantage tells it's ready.

Cooldowns come from the local client's clocks. Shared ability timers show resource names together. The job-special timer doesn't assume a one-hour or two-hour length. Vantage doesn't work out charge counts, so a charged ability may be usable before its full timer ends. For items, Vantage covers eligible enchanted equipment you're wearing now, including the equip delay. It doesn't scan all your bags or track ordinary consumables. Items with no charges left don't announce that they're ready.

When Vantage loads, it knows the time left on a cooldown that's already running, but not its full length. When Vantage sees a cooldown start, bars use the first time left it saw. A projected countdown that reaches zero waits for fresh word from the client. Missing or unreadable slots stay uncertain, and they don't set off a ready alert.

On **Alerts**, set **Alert type** to **Recast**. **Make an alert for a recast**, on the Recasts panel's **Show** tab, opens Alerts with a new recast alert to set up. Pick a spell, ability timer or item, or **All recasts**, then set **Trigger** to **Recast started**, **Recast ready** or **Time running low**. The usual visual notices, row emphasis, local chat, optional sounds, conditions, cooldowns and test controls apply. Percent thresholds need an original length that Vantage saw. Loading, zoning or changing jobs sets a quiet baseline instead of replaying old starts. **Recast ready** tells you the cooldown ended. It doesn't promise the MP, charges, range or anything else you need to use it.

Categories you aren't using stop polling, unless an alert that's on needs them or their settings catalog is open. Active categories sample at most every 100 ms. Countdowns move on between samples without extra client reads.

### Recast tints

A recast row tints while it couldn't be used. It does this in every layout (bars, expanded bars, icons and text) and with one panel or several. A tint goes under the row's text, so the countdown always shows. Each tint starts off. Each has its own tick and color in the **Tint recasts I can't use right now** fold on the panel's **Show** tab, and that fold shows while a recast panel shows spells or abilities.

- **Spells while my MP is too low** turns a spell row blue while your MP is below the spell's cost. The cost is the era one Phoenix uses. Phoenix's era settings change the cost of 54 elemental nukes and of Raise II and III and Reraise II and III, so those use Phoenix's numbers. Every other spell uses the client's. Manafont stops the tint. Songs and ninjutsu cost no MP. Vantage doesn't count gear that lowers the cost.
- **Spells while Silenced or Muted** turns spell rows grey and shows a lock. The lock's time left comes from your own status timer. When the game gives no time, the lock shows with none.
- **Abilities under Amnesia** does the same to ability rows while Amnesia is on you.

In Salvage, Omerta locks every spell and Impairment locks abilities, as Phoenix puts them on at the entrance. In Nyzul Isle they stop only some kinds of spell or move, and the game doesn't tell the client which. So they lock nothing there. Hover a row to read why it's tinted. Item rows never tint. With **Show samples** on, the sample Cure IV row shows the MP tint, and the sample Haste and Provoke rows show the spell and ability locks. That way you can see each color, for the tints that are on.

With no icon on the row, the lock and its time go just before the countdown, and the spell's name stops short of them.

### Ready pulse

When a recast of a minute or more becomes ready, its icon grows and fades once. The pulse starts off. To turn it on, tick **Ready pulse** on the main page. It has an editor of its own, which **Edit** on that row opens, and so does **Edit the ready pulse** on the Recasts panel's **Show** tab. **On**, at the top of it, turns the pulse on too. You set it up on its **Show** tab:

- **Only recasts of at least** is 60 seconds at first. A recast's length is its full cooldown if Vantage saw it start, or else the most time it saw left.
- **Start early** sets how many seconds before ready it pulses. It then pulses that early, once, and not again at ready.
- For **Pulse spot**, **On its row** pulses where a recast panel shows that recast as it becomes ready, over the panels. A recast its panel doesn't show then, because of **When to show**, its filters or **Most shown**, gets no pulse, and nor does one whose panel is off. If no recast panel is on, the tab says so and has **Use a spot of its own**. **A spot of its own** pulses at a spot you place, whether or not a panel shows the recast. It also pulses there while the timer panels are paused or hidden by **Hide timer panels while I type**. The spot starts locked, and holding Shift shows where it is. To move it, turn off **Locked** at the top of the editor, then hold Shift and drag it. Or set its **Across** and **Down** on **Size and place**, which shows while the pulse has a spot of its own. **Show a test pulse** plays one with a sample icon.
- The tab also has **Pulse size**, **Pulse color** and **Pulse for**.
- **Never pulse for** lets you pick recasts that never pulse. **Pulse again** takes one off the list.

The pulse only draws. It takes no clicks and makes no sound. Recast alerts on **Alerts** still give notices and sounds.

## The Filters tab

Open a panel's **Filters** tab.

**Effect** picks an effect, and reads **Choose an effect** until you do. The box at the top of its list finds one by name, and typing an effect's number finds it too. The lists hold the effects that can exist on Phoenix. An effect you already picked stays listed while it's saved. **Show only** makes an allow-list when at least one effect is ticked. **Hide here** takes priority. **Pin first** keeps an effect above ordinary rows. **Rename it** changes the name that shows, but it's still the same effect. Hovering the effect shows the new name too.

**Add to My order** puts an effect on **My order**, the list right under **Sort** on the **Show** tab, where **Up**, **Down** and **Remove** arrange it. Effects you didn't list follow the ones you did. With nothing on the list, rows go by name and a line says so. **Group rows** on **Show** groups the rows by target or effect group when that's useful. Each effect can have its own **Color**. It shows on a bar, or on a countdown while **Color each countdown by how sure Vantage is of its time** is on.

## Alerts

**Alerts** on the main page starts on. **Edit** on that row opens its panel. On **Show**, after **Add**, **Haste ending**, **Sleep ending**, **Silenced** and **Shadows gone** each add a ready-made alert. **Recast ready** adds one for a recast you pick, and **New alert** adds one you set up yourself. Those two stay off until you choose what they watch, and a line in the alert says so. So does an alert that's on when you change its **Alert type**. Click a ready-made one again and it turns its alert back on and opens it. **Your alerts** lists them by name. The tick turns one on or off. **Edit** opens it below and **Done** closes it. **Copy** adds a copy. **Delete** takes it away, and Undo brings it back. **Your pet**, at the end of **Show**, has your pet's alerts (see [Pet](#pet)).

An open alert has **Alert name**, then **What**, **Who** and **When to alert**, which cover the effect, who to watch and when it fires. Then **Notice**, **Chat log**, **Bar** and **Sounds** cover what it does. **Effect** reads **Choose an effect**, or **Choose a recast**, until you pick one. **Only on these main jobs** is a fold at the end of **When to alert**. It limits the alert to the jobs you tick, and a line under the fold names them. **Flash the bar** shows while **Make the effect's bar stand out** is on. **Notice color** shows while **Show a notice** is on. It gives that alert's notice a text color of its own, so a Sleep alert can be red and a recast alert green. It reads **From Alerts** until you pick one, and **Use Alerts' color** takes it off again. Use **Test this alert** to try the selected output without applying a real effect.

**Quiet during cutscenes and while dead**, **Sound volume**, **Notice text size** and **Notice text color** are at the top of **Show**. Alerts has no **Look** tab. The size starts as **Same as Text size**, which follows **Text size** on the main page, and goes from 8 to 48. Bigger text makes a notice taller, and a long message still wraps inside **Width**. The color starts as the shared text color. An alert's own **Notice color** beats it. **Size and place** has **Width**, **Height**, **Notices shown at once** and where the notices go. **When to show** sets when they show, and while it hides them no alert plays at all, in chat or as a sound. There's no **Filters** tab, because each alert picks its own effects. **Reset all of this display** puts all of Alerts back the way it started, your alerts and the pet alerts too. That's why it asks first and says how many. `/vg reset alerts` does the same without asking, and Undo brings them back. `/vg mute` and **Mute alerts**, at the end of the window's top bar, mute notices and sounds. Then the **Alerts** row has **Unmute**, the top of the panel says `Alerts are muted, so none of them fires.`, and the top bar has **Unmute alerts**.

- **Effect gained:** Vantage sees an application, or a status that's newly there, after the quiet startup baseline.
- **Time running low:** a current timer crosses a threshold you picked. Seconds work when the time left is known. Percent needs a known original total. Switching **Threshold unit** to **Percent remaining** keeps any thresholds up to 100. If there aren't any, it uses 10.
- **Effect removed / wore off:** Vantage sees a removal, or a complete status snapshot shows the status is gone. It doesn't tell an effect that ran out from one that was cancelled, dispelled or removed some other way, unless the evidence does. Recasting a buff before it ends counts as a refresh and fires no **Effect removed / wore off** alert. That includes a Refresh recast on a party member.
- **Required effect missing:** an effect isn't in a status snapshot of the one the rule watches, and that snapshot is known to be complete. Choose a specific effect. An unknown or out-of-range state doesn't count as missing. An effect that's missing from the start has a short settling period before its first warning. **Required effect missing** rules can't prove an enemy lacks an effect Vantage hasn't seen.

Remaining-time alerts on anyone but you go by estimates. That's why their notices are marked estimated and can arrive after an effect ended early. Percent rules skip modeled duration ranges, because those don't give a known original total. If one update crosses multiple thresholds, you get the most urgent notice. Each threshold fires once for each application. **Repeat every** can remind you while the condition goes on, and **Minimum time between repeats** spaces those reminders out. A refresh clears stale notices. A reload, zoning, a death, rule edits and unmuting don't replay old threshold crossings. A repeat interval keeps going after an event, a death, a zone change or a mute. It counts from when that ended. With no repeat interval, Vantage tells you about a missing effect once each time it goes missing. That's also true when it goes missing during an event or a mute. A zone change or a pause of more than 10 seconds starts every rule over. So if an effect is still missing then, Vantage tells you again.

Visual notices and row emphasis are local. Vantage prints chat output only in your own client. Sounds are optional and start unselected. Put your WAV or MP3 files in the settings folder's `sounds` subfolder. Then refresh the list, select a file and test it. A file whose name is over 160 characters long or has `##` in it isn't listed, because a rule can't keep that name. Each rule can follow **Sound volume** or have its own. Bar emphasis can flash in a color you choose, for as long as you choose. Vantage limits warnings that go off at the same time to one sound per second. A rule at volume 0 plays no sound and leaves that one to another rule. It doesn't matter whether that volume is its own or the **Sound volume** it follows. Vantage doesn't bundle any audio files.

## Timer durations

**Timer durations** changes how long Vantage counts the effects it estimates. Those are the effects on mobs, party members and other players. Your own effects follow the game's timers and never change here. Click **Timer durations** in the bar at the top of the window to open it.

**Duration overrides** sets your own time for a spell, a job ability or an effect. Pick one with **Override applies to** and **What to override**, then click **Add duration override**, which shows once you've picked. **What to override** reads **Choose one** until then, and the box at the top of its list finds one by name. A new override starts at a fixed 60 seconds. **Change the time to** works on the one you picked under **Override to edit**. It can be **A fixed time** (**Fixed time**), **Add or take away seconds**, **A percent of the estimate**, or **Count up from when it landed**. That last one shows the time since it landed in place of a countdown. **Only on a mob or player named** keeps the override to one mob or player by its exact name, and **Use my target's name** fills that in. A rule for a spell or ability goes before one for its effect. A rule with a name goes before one without. An overridden time is still an estimate, and its details say so. A count up has no time left, so **Only effects with a known time** hides it and **Time running low** alerts skip it. It stays listed as long as the estimate it hides would.

Under **Learned durations**, **Learn from my casts** starts off. With it on, Vantage keeps the time from when it sees an effect of yours land on a mob or another player to its wear-off. The times go by spell and by mob name, and players share one time for each spell. Vantage keeps up to nine for each, the newest ones. Once there are enough times for **Casts before a learned time is used** (3), your casts of that spell on that mob count down from the middle one. Their details say the time is learned and estimated. Other people's casts keep the usual estimate. An override still goes first. With learning off, the times stay saved but aren't used. Phoenix checks for expiry every 0.4 seconds, so a learned time can read up to 0.4 seconds long. A resisted cast is shorter. That's why Vantage uses the middle one. The client can't tell a mob's family, so learning goes by name.

Vantage learns nothing from these ends: a removal it saw, an end with no wear-off (a death, Dispel, a zone change), a sleep woken by a hit or running beside a damage tick, a Bind that took a hit, and another caster's copy over yours. Recasting your own starts its clock again. Phoenix sends you the wear-off just before the hit, removal or new copy that ended it, so Vantage waits a second before it learns a wear-off. Any of those in that second keeps it out.

Vantage saves at most 128 learned times in each character's settings file. **Clear learned durations** forgets them in one Undo step. They aren't settings, so every other Undo and Redo step, **Reset this page**, `/vg reset`, profiles and shared presentation keep this character's own. Profiles and shared presentation carry the overrides and the learning switches. `/vg reset durations` resets this page.

## Arrange on screen

**Arrange on screen** lets you place every display at once. Turn it on with `/vg edit` or **Arrange on screen** under **Moving** on the main page. Every display on the screen gets an outline, with its name just above it. A bar at the top of the screen holds the one you pick. While the settings window is open up there, the bar sits at the bottom of the screen instead, so the window's title and top row stay clear. When the window reaches the bottom too, the bar comes in front of it and stays in front when you click the window. Overhead icons sit over heads in the world, so they aren't in Arrange on screen. A display that's off has no outline until you turn it on. In Arrange on screen, a display shows even when it's empty, out of combat, while you type, or where its **When to show** choices would hide it. That way you can place it. The bar says when those choices would hide it, and its outline's name ends in `(hidden here now)`.

- Click a display to pick it, or pick it from the **Display** list in the bar. Hold Shift and drag to move it. It goes exactly where you drag it, unless **Snap while moving** is on and the display snaps.
- A lock only stops Shift and drag outside Arrange on screen. Here a locked display moves like any other. Party list's groups share one lock. A group beside the game's list moves by its offset from the game's party window, and one in a panel of its own moves on the screen.
- **Snap while moving** is in the bar, and it's the same tick as the one under **Moving** on the main page. It starts off. Turning it on while no display is ticked to snap ticks every one. While it's on, the bar has the **Snapping** fold. In it, **This display snaps** is for the display you picked. **Show the grid** and **Grid**, the grid's size, come next. After **Line up with** are five switches: **Grid**, **Screen edges**, **Other displays**, **Party list** and **Chat log**. Last is **Snap distance**. Each tick and each turn of a switch is one Undo step.
- A display that snaps does it as you drag it. That works in Arrange on screen, or with Shift outside it once the display is unlocked, and it includes Party list. As the switches choose, it snaps within **Snap distance** to the grid, the screen's center and edges, the other displays' edges and middles (not those that follow it), the game's party list and the chat log. It snaps to the last two where Vantage can read them. A resize snaps the edge you pull while the edge across from it stays put. The line it snapped to shows while you drag, and so does the grid while **Show the grid** is on. Both go away when you let go. With every switch after **Line up with** off, nothing snaps.
- While the bar or a picked display has focus, the arrow keys move it 1 px, or 10 with Shift. Arrange on screen takes the keys only then, through ImGui. The bar doesn't take them when it opens, so click it or a display first. When it comes in front of the settings window, it takes them. Click the game to give them back. Arrow presses less than about half a second apart count as one move.
- **Moves with** in the bar says what the display follows. **Size and place** opens that tab, where you type its spot or pick what it moves with. A number you type in **Grid** or **Snap distance** takes effect when you press Enter or click away. Typing alone changes nothing. Pressing Escape while you type in one puts its number back and keeps Arrange on screen on. The next Escape ends it. Their - and + buttons take effect at once.
- Each move is one Undo step. A move is a drag or a run of arrow presses. The bar has **Undo** and **Redo** too.
- **Done**, `/vg edit` or Escape while the bar has focus ends Arrange on screen. So does **Done arranging** at the top of the main page. Escape with the **Display** list open only closes the list. Every display stays where it is. In Arrange on screen, a click picks a display.

**Moves with** on a display's **Size and place** tab keeps the display beside something. That can be a spot on the screen (a corner, an edge or the center), another display, the game's party list (your party's window) or the chat log. A spot keeps the display's same corner or edge **Across from it** and **Down from it** of that **Spot**. That way it stays put when the screen size changes. The other choices put it on a **Side** of what it follows, with a **Gap**. It lines up with its near end, middle or far end (**Line up**), and **Along the side** moves it along that side. In Arrange on screen, moving a display that moves with something keeps what it moves with and changes those distances, up to 300 pixels from it and 2000 along the side. Past that, it says so. A move starts from where you see the display and stops at the screen's edges. To place it freely, set **Moves with** to **Nothing**. It stays where it is, and that becomes its **Across** and **Down**. A display can't follow one that follows it back.

The chat log and the game's party list sit at the bottom of the screen. If you pick one while **Side** is on Below, **Side** moves to Above or Left. A spot on the screen starts where the display is now.

The display goes to its own **Across** and **Down** while what it follows isn't on the screen (an empty or hidden display, or a party list or chat log Vantage can't read). It also goes there when there's no room on that side of the chat log, and a line on that tab says so. Outside Arrange on screen, a Shift drag ends **Moves with**. **Nothing** under **Moves with** and **Put it back where it started** are one Undo step each. Profiles and shared presentation carry **Moves with**, **Snap while moving**, which displays snap and the grid. Placement sharing carries what each panel moves with. `/vg reset layout` resets **Snap while moving**, which displays snap, the grid, snapping and **Moves with** on every display. `/vg reset moves_with` resets only **Moves with**, and `/vg reset edit_mode` resets only **Snap while moving**, which displays snap, the grid and snapping.

## When to show

**When to show** sets where and when a display shows. Every display has it as its last tab. The ready pulse has it while the pulse is on, and on Party cooldowns it's the list's. The conditions are:

- **Main job** and **Support job** are folds, each titled with the jobs you picked. **Show it for** is **Only these**, which matches any of the jobs you check, or **All but these**, which matches every other job.
- **Level**, **From** and **To** go by your main job's level as the game shows it, which is lowered under Level Sync or a level cap. They go from 1 to 75, and 75 in **To** leaves no top.
- **Level Sync** shows it only while you're Level Synced, or only while you're not.
- **Where** is a fold too, with **Show it in** for **Only these** or **All but these** over the kinds you check. **Towns**, **Fields** and **Dungeons** are Phoenix's own zone types. The ferries to Selbina and Mhaura and the battlefield arenas have no type and count as **Other zones**. The airships and the pirate ferries are fields. **Battlefields** is the Battlefield status. The game gives you that status when you register for a battlefield and keeps it until you leave the battlefield. So a fight in Ghelsba Outpost is a dungeon and a battlefield. **Dynamis**, **Limbus**, **Assault**, **Salvage**, **Nyzul Isle** and **The Ashu Talif** are those zones. **Mog House** comes from the zone you entered. While you're in one, the town around it doesn't count. After `/addon reload` in a Mog House, Vantage knows it at the next zone.
- **Party** has **Solo**, **In a party** and **In an alliance**, as the game's party list shows it.
- **Fighting** shows it only while you're engaged or only while you're not. **Pet** shows it only with a pet out or only without one. The Pet display shows only with a pet out, so it has the **Pet** row only while a pick saved before is still on it.

Every condition you set must hold. A condition with several picks holds for any of them. A display with none always shows. The tab says the display's conditions in plain words, and where you are now, such as `Now: WHM 75 / BLM, in a dungeon, in a party, not engaged, no pet.` Once you set one, it also says whether they hide the display right now. Conditions hide only the display, and tracking and alerts go on. Alerts is the exception. While its own When to show hides it, no alert plays, not its chat line or its sound either. Show samples and Arrange on screen show a display anyway, but Arrange on screen doesn't place overhead icons. The ready pulse has a test pulse in place of samples. **Reset this tab** on that tab clears them in one Undo step. Profiles and shared presentation carry them, and `/vg reset conditions` clears them all.

## Import and export

**Import and export**, on **Profiles and sharing**, copies part of your settings as text for a friend to import into their Vantage. Pick what to send under **What to export**: **Whole profile**, **One display** (picked under **Display to export**), **Where everything is**, **Hide list**, **Alert rules** or **A saved profile** (picked under **Profile to export**). Then click **Copy**, and it goes to the clipboard and nowhere else. Or click **Save to a file** to keep it as a `.txt` file in `config/addons/vantage/shared/`, named after what it holds, such as `Hide list.txt`.

An export looks like `VG:1:1a2b3c4d:{...}`. That's a short prefix, the version, a checksum and then the settings as JSON. It holds settings only, never character or entity ids or file paths. Text that looks like a file path, with a backslash or a drive such as `C:/`, is left empty, even a title such as `HP\MP`, and the page says how many. Vantage takes out the names of your own characters, and your Windows name too. It looks for them in what you typed, such as titles, labels and rule names, and in sound, font and icon set names. So a setting or a skin that shares its name with your character stays. A whole profile looks for every character with a settings folder on this PC, and the other kinds look for the one you're playing. Vantage leaves out a hide rule that names a character, yours or anyone's, an alert rule for one party member, and a duration override only on one of your characters. It leaves out the whole rule, since without that name it would reach everyone. A duration override only on a mob keeps its name. The page says when it does. No export holds a display's lock. One display's export also leaves out that display's switch and its samples.

To use one, copy it, then click **Import from clipboard**. Vantage refuses an export from another version or one whose checksum doesn't match. It also refuses one longer than 256 KB or nested too deeply, and one that holds a setting Vantage doesn't have. Vantage cleans the entries of a list in it, such as the effects a panel shows, the same way it cleans a saved setting's. It reads the data with its strict JSON reader and never runs it. When Vantage can read an export, it shows what the export holds and what **Import and add** and **Import and replace** would change. **Import and add** keeps every setting you changed and takes the rest from the export, or adds its rules after yours and skips any you already have. A display's spot counts as one setting, and so does what it moves with, so a display you moved only across keeps its spot. **Import and replace** puts what the export holds in place of yours. Either is one Undo step. When nothing in the export differs from yours, Vantage says so and nothing changes. Both tidy every value the same way Vantage tidies a saved setting, and both keep your own locks, samples, diagnostics settings and chat colors. One display's export keeps that display's switch yours. A whole profile brings over which displays are on. Sound files aren't in an export, so a rule that plays one needs the same file in your `sounds` folder.

You can copy an export from Discord or chat as it was posted. Vantage skips code marks, quote marks, line breaks and any text around it, and the checksum still catches a change inside it. With two exports in one paste, it reads the first. An export of another version, or one that holds a setting this Vantage doesn't have, was made with a newer Vantage. The page says "This was made with a newer Vantage. Update Vantage to read it." To keep a whole profile as a new saved profile instead, use **Save as a new profile** under it (see Appearance and profiles).

### Your own icon sets

You can use buff and debuff icons of your own. Vantage ships no pictures. It only reads the ones you put there. Put each set in a folder of its own in the settings folder's `icons` subfolder, which Vantage makes the first time it looks. **Open icons folder**, beside **Reload icons**, opens it in File Explorer. In exclusive fullscreen, File Explorer may open behind the game, so Alt+Tab to it. The folder's name is the set's name. So a set called Bright is `config/addons/vantage/icons/Bright/`. Vantage reads folders, not zip files, so a set that comes as a zip needs extracting first. In File Explorer, right-click the zip, pick **Extract All** and keep the folder it suggests, which is named after the zip. If the zip held a folder of its own, that leaves one folder inside another, as in `icons/Bright/Bright/`. While the outer folder has no pictures named by number, Vantage reads the set from the inner folder and names it after the outer one. In the set's folder, name each picture by the number of the effect it's for, such as `33.png` for Haste or `40.png` for Protect. A name may go on after the number with a space, a dash, an underscore or a dot, as in `33 Haste.png`, and a leading zero is fine, as in `033.png`. A pack named any other way needs its files renamed first. Vantage reads PNG, DDS, BMP, TGA and JPG pictures of up to 1 MB and 1024 px each way. A picture that isn't square is stretched to a square.

Each set is a choice after **Original FFXI** and **XI View** in **Icons**, under **Artwork** on the main page, and in **Party buff icons** on Party list's **Look** tab. **Icons** sets the icons on the timer panels, Overhead icons, Phantom Roll and Pet, and on the recast panels while **Icons show** is **The effect it gives**. **Party buff icons** sets Party list's own. You can switch between any of these at any time. It changes at once, with no reload, and you can always switch back. Picking a **Skin** puts the skin's icons back, as it does after any other pick, so pick the set again after one, or use Undo. An effect a set has no picture for shows the Original FFXI icon, and Ashita's own picture if that's missing too. A set's picture also wins over the item picture a few monster-only effects show. Recast icons don't use sets.

Vantage makes each icon the first time it shows, a few each frame, so a set's icons can take a moment to come in. For most displays it shrinks a picture to 64 px at most. Party list makes each icon at the size it draws, up to 128 px, so a bigger picture stays sharp there.

**Reload icons**, under **Icons** and on Party list's **Look** tab, reads the folder again. A set you added shows in the list, and pictures you changed show the next time they're drawn, all without reloading Vantage. A line under the pick says when some pictures in a set can't be used. Vantage tries one it couldn't make again every 5 seconds, so a picture you were still saving comes in on its own. `/vg report` saves, under `icon_sets`, how many pictures each set has, each picture and folder Vantage left out, with why, and any size Party list couldn't make a picture at. For a set read from a folder inside its own, `inner_folder` names that folder. A folder named like a built-in choice, with a name longer than 64 characters, with ## in it or ending in #, or with no pictures named by number in it or in one folder inside it, isn't a set.

Vantage can't open zip, 7z or rar files. While the icons folder holds one with no folder of its name beside it, a line under **Icons** and under **Party buff icons** says to extract it, and **Reload icons** says so too. The same goes for pictures put straight in the icons folder instead of in a set's folder. A zip with a folder of its name beside it counts as extracted. If you extracted one into a folder with another name, delete the zip and press **Reload icons**. `/vg report` lists both under `icon_sets`, as `archives` and `loose`. A folder with no pictures named by number but several folders inside that have them isn't read either, as Vantage can't tell which is the set. The same line says to move each into the icons folder as a set of its own.

A change in the letter case of a folder's name alone keeps the set, as Windows finds the folder either way. If a set's folder is gone, or renamed, the pick keeps its name, with **(not in the icons folder)** after it. The game's icons show, and a line says so. Put the folder back and press **Reload icons**, and the set comes back. An export keeps the set's name but not its pictures. On a PC without that set, the game's icons show the same way.

Client bitmap fonts apply to timers and alert notices. At startup, Vantage builds this window's matching fonts locally from those glyph shapes and caches them under the settings folder's `cache/client-fonts` subfolder. If that fails, this window uses its plain font, and a line under **Font** says so. When the client file a font comes from changes, its new font replaces the old one in that folder. If a client file can't make a font, Vantage notes it there and doesn't try that file again until the file changes. Those fonts keep the soft grey pixels at the edges of the game's letters, and Vantage asks Ashita to draw them pixel for pixel, with none of the sideways smoothing small fonts get. They leave out the letters' dark shadow, because this window draws its text in one color, so its text looks a little different from the bitmap timers. Bitmap HUD text uses the original client texture with whole-pixel positioning. The client choices cover English text. Unsupported characters use a fallback glyph. Original artwork uses fixed frame corners. Its color controls tint the artwork, and white keeps the texture colors as they are. Custom gradients use the regular bar drawing. **Size** stays adjustable to suit your client interface. This skin uses the actual client artwork, but exact game text spacing and compositing aren't guaranteed. Menus keep the editor's working title bar, resize handles, inputs, and color picker. Their interactions and controls aren't the game's native menu engine.

Choose Phoenix, Classic FFXI, Original FFXI, XI View, Frameless, High contrast, Ember, Mint, Lavender, or Colorblind safe, then adjust individual colors. The 17 font choices are Ashita (Agave), Segoe UI, Arial, Tahoma, Verdana, Calibri, Consolas, Segoe UI Semibold, Tahoma Bold, Segoe UI Bold, Verdana Bold, Arial Bold, Calibri Bold, Trebuchet MS Bold, Bahnschrift, Consolas Bold, and Georgia Bold. Fonts use available installed resources and fall back when a selected face is unavailable. Put custom TTF or OTF files in the settings folder's `fonts` subfolder, then run `/addon reload vantage` to add them to the list. Vantage leaves a file out of the list if it's cut short, damaged or not a font, and `/vg report` says why. Custom font files stay outside the release package.

One font serves every display and this window. Name, countdown, and detail text can use their own colors. Outlines and shadows are optional.

### Countdown text

Vantage copies it as a whole profile export without loading it. Your screen doesn't change and nothing goes into Undo. Send it in a message, or click **Save to a file** to keep it as a `.txt` file named after the profile in `config/addons/vantage/shared/`. Vantage makes that folder when it needs it. It keeps only letters, digits, spaces, hyphens and underscores in the file name, and it leaves your Windows name and the names of the characters on this PC out of it. It never writes over a file that's already there. A second save of Raid becomes `Raid 2.txt`. A whole profile is long, so Discord may turn one into a file named `message.txt`. It opens like any other file.

To use one a friend sent, copy it and click **Import from clipboard**. Or put their file in your `shared` folder, click **Import from a file** and pick it. Vantage fills in the name after **Save it as**. It's "Imported" for one from the clipboard. For a file, it's the file's name without `.txt`, cut to 40 characters. If that name is taken, Vantage adds " 2" and so on. Type another name if you like. If a profile of yours already has that name, Vantage asks for another, since this never replaces a profile. **Save as a new profile** saves it without loading it and picks it in the list, so **Load** is one click. It keeps your own locks, party members, watch list, job links, diagnostics settings and chat colors. When its displays were placed on a screen of another size, Vantage says so and ticks **Keep my own display positions**. That keeps where your displays sit and their sizes. Untick it to take the profile's. **Clear** drops it and saves nothing. You can also bring in a file or clipboard export of any kind with **Import and add** or **Import and replace** (see Import and export). Vantage never deletes your files in the shared folder.

**Start over**, at the end of the page, has two buttons and the **Reset one part** fold. **Put every display back where it started** sends every display to its first spot on this screen, following nothing and locked. It keeps each display's size and looks, and your choices. **Reset all settings** makes every display the way a fresh install has it, locked and in its first spot. It keeps profiles, job links, watched enemies and learned times. It also turns **Share settings across my characters** off. Each asks first, under its button. Each is one Undo step, and Undo brings all of it back. **Reset one part** puts one part of every display back as it started. It has **Reset how everything looks**, **Reset When to show on every display**, **Reset the effect lists of every timer panel**, **Reset Moves with on every display**, **Reset every timer panel's size and place** and **Reset chat line colors**. Each of those asks first too, and Undo takes it back.

**Share settings across my characters**, under **Across characters**, keeps your settings the same on each character. **What to share** shows while it's on. **Where the panels and Party list are** shares panel geometry. **Where things are and how they look** also shares panel styling, general filters, colors, visibility, notice presentation, the overhead icon settings, the recast tints, the ready pulse, the party cooldown settings and the Phantom Roll and pet displays. Each character keeps its own lock of the pulse spot, the cooldown list and those two displays, and their samples. Named profiles and shared presentation include the separate/combined recast choice and its category filters. Placement-only sharing keeps those choices local. Character member selections, watched IDs, alert rules, job links, and editor navigation stay personal. Which party list groups are on, and their lock, also stay with each character. Sharing can include their sizing and offsets. Whether overhead icons are on, and their samples, stay with each character too. Undo and Redo restore settings changes, including Shift editor placement. Navigating the editor doesn't use up display undo steps, but Undo puts back what **Reset all settings** changed in the settings window. Appearance reset keeps filters and alerts. Layout reset keeps effect choices and custom labels. Turning sharing on or changing its scope loads the existing shared settings first. Later edits publish only when a setting in the shared scope changes. Personal editor and alert changes can't replace a newer shared layout. If two characters move panels within moments of each other, both keep their moves. Undo after another character's change keeps that change. Sharing placement with a character who shares presentation keeps the shared appearance. If Vantage can't read `shared_layout.json`, each character still saves its own settings and keeps trying to share its change. It tries less often while the file stays unreadable, and merges the change in once the file is fixed. A change that still isn't shared when you switch character or unload Vantage stays with that character, and it's shared the next time that character logs in.

**Chat line colors**, a fold under **While playing** on the main page, sets the colors of Vantage's own lines in the chat log, as the game's color numbers. **Chat bracket color**, **Chat name color** and **Chat message color** go from 1 to 255. They skip 10 and 13, since those end a chat line. **Print a sample line** prints one in your chat log that only you see. `/vg set chat_colors.tag_brackets 81`, `chat_colors.tag_word` and `chat_colors.replies` set them from the chat line too. `/vg chatsample` prints a sample, and `/vg reset chat_colors` or **Reset chat line colors** puts them back. **Copy shown timers** and **Copy timer details**, on the **Show** tab of each timer and recast panel, put text on your clipboard. With nothing showing, they say so and leave the clipboard as it is. **Record recent events** keeps a limited history of observed events, and **Export report** saves the current evidence to the settings folder's `reports/latest.json`. Reports stay local and may include observed character or target names. Recording starts off.

## If something looks wrong

Turn on **Show samples** to tell a hidden or filtered panel from missing game data. Check that the panel is ticked on the main page, then look at its **Filters** tab and its **When to show** tab. A blank caster or an effect with no countdown is normal when Vantage didn't observe that information. If it's still wrong, `/vg report` saves what Vantage found to `reports/latest.json` in the settings folder.

Settings are JSON, never executable Lua. Vantage keeps invalid existing JSON instead of writing over it, and reports it by file name. An empty file, or one with nothing but blank space, counts as no settings, so Vantage starts from the defaults and saves over it. Keep a copy before you repair a damaged settings file, and save it as UTF-8. A byte order mark is fine, but Vantage can't read UTF-16, which Windows PowerShell 5.1 writes by default. Vantage reports disk or audio failures locally. A fresh reload starts with a quiet baseline. It can't recover old enemy applications that the client never sent to this session.

## After a game update

When it loads, Vantage uses short byte patterns to find a few things in the game client. Following the chat log uses the chat window and menu scale. The visibility options use the event, interface and map flags. The buffs beside the party list use the game's party list rows and the Treasure Pool window. Your own countdowns use the game clock, and overhead icons use the point the game hangs each name from. Item recasts use Ashita's item data library, which looks for patterns of its own. Right after a game update, one of them may not be found yet. Then only that part stops. Depending on which part it is, your own buffs show without a countdown, item recasts turn off, following the chat log is unavailable, Party list isn't drawn, overhead icons go back to their guessed heights, or the visibility options stop working. **Replace the game's buff icons** looks for the game's buff drawing and its + menu the first time it's used, and if it doesn't find them just as it knows them, the game draws its own buff icons and a line under the tick says why. **Replace the game's pet bar** does the same with the game's pet bar. Everything else keeps working. Chat says so once when the game clock or those flags are missing, and `/vg report` says which part is missing. The Recasts panel also says when item recasts are off. There's nothing to turn off. A Vantage or Ashita update that knows the new client brings it back.
